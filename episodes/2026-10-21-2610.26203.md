The paper is Focused Omni-Modal GUI Agents, from Fuyao Zhang, Wenwen He, and Jiaming Zhang, at Nanyang Technological University, with Longtao Huang at Alibaba Group. The central claim is that a GUI agent operating over a stream of omni-modal observations does not fail because it is shown too much, but because it is given no signal about what matters at the current step. The authors call the resulting compounding failure Sequential Distraction: an action the agent takes re-enters the next observation and the next history, so a per-step misread does not end with the current prediction. It feeds forward. By the fourth step of an episode, the frozen Qwen2.5-Omni backbone has lost roughly half of its Type Match score, and the paper's fix, a small adapter called FocusGUI, retains about eighty-seven percent of it.

The motivation is laid out as an empirical analysis, and it is the part of the paper worth keeping. The authors ablate individual modalities from the input of a Qwen2.5-Omni model during multi-step interaction and measure the change in the log-likelihood of the ground-truth action. If removing a modality drops the likelihood, that modality was useful at that step; if it raises the likelihood, the modality was actually interfering. Running this across episodes, they find the dominant modality switches across forty-five point three percent of adjacent steps, and ninety-four percent of episodes contain at least one such switch. So the relevant source is not a fixed property of the observation; it is a property of the step. Within a single step the evidence is also sparse: the target element occupies a median of just zero point eight seven percent of the screen area, and video content changes by a median of only seven point four one percent within a step. The implication is that the decoder is being forced to rediscover, from scratch, a relevance pattern that shifts almost every step, with no explicit signal of what changed.

The obvious remedy, and the one most existing methods take, is subtraction: crop the screen down to a region of interest, or otherwise give the model less. The paper argues this misaligns with streaming interaction in two ways. The decisive signal can come from audio, video, or device state at any moment, channels that spatial cropping never touches. And even when the signal is on screen, the crudely cropped background often still contains the contextual evidence you need to ground accurate coordinates and typed arguments. So the fix should change how the decoder reads the observation, not what it is given.

FocusGUI realizes that principle with a small adapter sitting at the action boundary of a frozen omni-modal backbone. The backbone and its full multimodal input are left completely untouched. From the same observation, FocusGUI decomposes it into separable perceptual channels, scores each channel against a decision reference that is recomputed from the whole observation at every step, and compresses the prioritized channels into a compact focus latent. That latent is expanded into a residual and added, as a zero-initialized additive term, to a small set of K existing embedding positions immediately before the action begins generating. Because the injection is additive and starts at zero, the adapter begins as the identity map on the frozen backbone's input and gradually learns observation-conditioned deviations. Nothing is appended to the prompt, nothing is removed from the observation, and no backbone parameter is updated.

The per-step scoring is what makes it adaptive. Each channel is mapped into a shared dimension through a source-specific projector, and a decision reference is derived from the integrated representation of the complete observation. Each channel's compatibility with that reference is turned into a normalized priority, a softmax over the channels, and the weighted sum becomes the input to a lower-dimensional focus projection. Crucially, both the channels and the reference are recomputed at every interaction step, and the scoring carries no state across steps. Drift is produced one step at a time, so removing the corruption at each step removes its accumulation. That is the direct answer to Sequential Distraction: you do not need to remember or track, you just re-prioritize at each step.

Training uses next-action supervision alone. The loss is the standard negative log-likelihood on the action tokens, the action primitive and its arguments, computed by the frozen backbone's language head on the modified embeddings. Gradients flow back through the frozen decoder into the adapter parameters, but no backbone weight is touched. There is no annotation saying which modality matters at which step; the entire focus pathway is learned from the action labels. The default configuration is deliberately small: a shared channel dimension of one hundred twenty-eight, an action-sufficient focus dimension of sixty-four, four residual slots, a zero-initialized residual at scale one, and dropout of zero point zero five. On the Qwen backbone the adapter has about one point four four five million trainable parameters against eight point nine three two billion frozen parameters in the thinker, a trainable proportion of roughly zero point zero one six percent.

The headline result, averaged across four omni-modal backbones, is that FocusGUI raises overall Type Match from thirty-one point zero percent to sixty-four point two percent and Exact Match from eleven point two percent to thirty-three point five percent over the frozen backbone, a gain of plus thirty-three point two in Type Match and a three times multiplier in Exact Match. On Qwen2.5-Omni specifically, the frozen backbone sits at forty-nine point six one Type Match and FocusGUI reaches seventy-one point six five, with Exact Match jumping from twelve point six zero to forty point one six. That last number, forty point one six, is the striking one because Exact Match requires the action type and every argument to be correct, which is where grounding errors live. Against the strongest existing enhancement method in their table, FocusGUI still reaches about one point nine times the Type Match, and beats the best method by plus thirty point one. The gain pattern also matches their own account: the improvement grows with interaction depth, exactly where Sequential Distraction is predicted to bite.

The theoretical section is more than decoration. They trace how the focus latent reaches the action logits through the frozen decoder and show it acts through two distinct pathways. One pathway changes what the decoder reads at a position, and that is sufficient to flip the action primitive between categories. The other pathway reallocates attention mass without adding content, and it is the only route by which arguments can be corrected. This is also the argument for the additive form: if you replaced the embeddings rather than adding to them, you would overwrite the K most recent, most heavily attended context positions with content of at most the focus dimension, erasing their value as evidence. So the residual has to stay additive, and the injection window has to be positioned before the action boundary so the signal reaches all subsequent action tokens.

Where I would be skeptical before adopting this. The four backbones are a small and heterogeneous set, and one of them, VITA, sits at very low absolute scores to begin with, so the relative multipliers there are inflated by a weak base. The benchmark is a GUI suite of their own construction across five task categories, and the Sequential Distraction analysis, while clean, is measured on a single backbone, Qwen2.5-Omni, and then generalized. The gains are large enough to take seriously, but I would re-verify them on a backbone I control and on an out-of-distribution device, because the whole method is a learned re-prioritization and its ceiling is set by how well the frozen backbone already separates the channels. The engineering gotcha is that this is not a drop-in: the adapter is per-backbone, the residual window position depends on where action generation begins in your token schema, and you must keep the injection additive or you break argument grounding. It is a genuinely useful, cheap mechanism, and the zero-initialized additive design is the part worth stealing for any frozen-model adapter you build.
