AutoDesign: Meta-Harness Optimization for Long-Horizon Agentic Design, by Yaxin Luo, Haobin Jiang, Jialv Zou, and colleagues, a fourteen-author collaboration spanning Meituan, MBZUAI, Huazhong University of Science and Technology, Peking University, Tsinghua University, The Chinese University of Hong Kong, and Shanghai Jiao Tong University. The paper's premise is that the harness around a language model, the prompts, tools, runtime, and evaluation loop that turn a source document into a finished artifact, should be an optimization target in its own right, distinct from model weights. Rather than hand-tuning that scaffold, AutoDesign runs a meta-level optimizer: a coding agent recursively rewrites the design harness itself based on rollout feedback, under an acceptance gate that blocks regressions. The team instantiates this on academic paper-to-poster generation and ships two artifacts worth your attention: DesignHarness, the evolved poster-generation system, and PosterBench, a frozen hundred-paper evaluation protocol with controlled tracks.

The formulation is clean. A design harness H is the system surrounding a fixed model that maps a multimodal source x and context c to a human-facing artifact y through an execution trajectory. The objective is the expected quality of produced artifacts under an evaluator R meta, and the meta-harness searches for the harness that maximizes it while model parameters stay fixed throughout. H is decomposed into five functional components: context and memory, meaning source management, prompts, skills, reusable assets, and persistent state; tools and specifications covering editable artifact specs for layout, typography, and provenance; execution runtime for authoring, rendering, validating, and exporting; orchestration covering task routing, attempt budgets, loop control, candidate selection, fallback, and finalization; and evaluation and feedback, which combines rule-based validation with model-based critique. The inner loop is a designer-critic pair: a designer module generates or revises an artifact at each refinement step given prior artifact and feedback, the critic evaluates and returns feedback, and repeated cycles produce an execution trajectory. The outer loop then improves the harness across tasks in four stages per iteration: rollout on a training set of design tasks, evaluation by a fixed evaluator, an update proposal from an optimizer agent, and an acceptance decision. The optimizer runs as one coding agent assuming two roles sequentially. In planner role it dispatches parallel subagents to inspect trajectories and scores, synthesizes recurrent failure evidence, and writes an update plan naming the observed failure modes, the component to modify, and intended changes. In code-editor role it implements those changes. Two restrictions matter for credit assignment: each outer-loop iteration may touch exactly one of the five components, though multiple files within it are allowed, and no tree search over harness variants is maintained, just a single active harness plus an optimization record storing checkpoints, trajectories, scores, plans, diffs, and accept-or-reject decisions as persistent context for future iterations. The acceptance gate is the anti-overfitting mechanism: a candidate replaces the current harness only if training-set performance strictly improves and independent development-set performance does not decline, and development results are never exposed to the optimizer when it constructs proposals.

Ingestion and validation inside the inner loop follow a fixed discipline worth copying. Paper ingestion extracts document metadata and section outlines, identifies key passages supporting main claims, records figures and tables with source locations, then compiles a content brief and a medium-specific artifact plan naming target format, claims to convey, and supporting visual evidence; every extracted element keeps a reference to its location in the source so statements can be traced back during revision, and this context is built once and reused across all refinement steps. The artifact stays as editable HTML throughout, so revisions are localized code edits instead of full regenerations, with PNG, PPTX, or MP4 renders used only for visual critique. The rule-based validator applies deterministic blocking checks covering unsafe or missing assets, broken provenance links between incorporated materials and their sources, severe overflow or overlap, and typographic or layout constraint violations; passing candidates finalize immediately, failing candidates get localized diagnostics plus non-blocking readouts on content coverage, information density, and numerical consistency with the source, then go to the critic VLM for rendered-property assessment before another designer pass.

PosterBench deserves its own description because most of the numbers depend on it. It contains a hundred-paper main track spanning AI and machine learning, biomedicine, climate, economics and policy, and physics and astronomy, plus PosterBench-mini, a ten-paper subset used for controlled ablations. Every system receives the same source paper and assets, renders output to a common format, and faces seven rubric dimensions scored zero to ten: faithfulness, coverage, density, visual evidence, layout, readability, and aesthetics, weighted ten, ten, fifteen, ten, twenty, twenty-five, ten respectively, so readability and layout carry half the weight. On top of the weighted rubric score sit record-level ceilings capping severe layout damage, insufficient viability, visible failures, and render-integrity violations; a standard gate caps any case at forty. Overall is the mean of capped scores, so dimension means do not recombine into it. The evaluator mixes programmatic spatial audits, OCR checks matching rendered numerals against source PDF numerals, overlap detection, and clipping audits with VLM rubric judgments. Critically, this benchmark evaluator is frozen and fully separate from R meta, the evaluator the outer loop optimizes against, so benchmark numbers cannot be gamed by the optimization process itself.

Now the numbers. On the main track, AutoDesign running Claude Code over Claude 4.8 scores 78.32, beating the commercial Claude Design at 70.87 by 7.45 points and OpenDesign at 69.45 by 8.87 points; the Codex over GPT-5.5 configuration reaches 77.97. Standalone coding agents trail: raw Claude Code scores 70.01, Doubao with Claude Code 61.14, GLM 52.22, Kimi 51.46, DeepSeek V4 Pro 46.01, and human-crafted workflows PosterGen, Any2Poster, and Paper2Poster land at 56.71, 49.09, and 44.61. The controlled ablation is the more honest read: across seven completed configurations, attaching DesignHarness lifts scores by 5.01 to 19.56 points, raising the average from 54.99 to 67.39. Largest gain is DeepSeek V4 Pro with Claude Code, up 19.56 points; smallest is Codex-GPT-5.5, already strong at 75.87 native, gaining 5.59 to 81.46 on mini. One mechanism favors multimodal models here: at each repair attempt the rendered preview of the previous attempt is fed back as visual context, letting the model see the clipped text or broken layout textual diagnostics miss. Cost data forms a Pareto frontier on mini: LongCat-2.0 hits 55.13 at about $0.27 per poster, Doubao Seed 2.1 Pro reaches 71.83 at $2.75, Claude 4.8 gives 74.56 at $7.63, GPT-5.5 tops out at 81.46 for $10.02, so Doubao buys 88 percent of the best score at 27 percent of the cost. Median runtimes range from 17 to 85 minutes depending on configuration. Human validation: eleven blind reviewers made 933 ranking judgments over 936 responses, and a Bradley-Terry fit puts AutoDesign's probability of beating a random alternative at 64.0 percent, interval 55.2 to 77.8, with head-to-head preference of 61.3 percent against raw Claude Code, 63.1 against OpenDesign, and 67.6 against Claude Design. Benchmark-human correlation at poster level is positive but weak, r equals 0.34, though for pairs separated by at least twenty PosterBench points reviewers side with the benchmark-preferred poster 74.4 percent of the time, which is the number that justifies trusting the aggregate ordering. The controlled tracks on mini isolate components cleanly: holding Claude Code and Claude 4.8 fixed and swapping only the design harness, AutoDesign scores 74.56 against OpenDesign at 70.36 and Claude Design at 66.83; fixing AutoDesign and GLM 5.2 while varying the coding harness puts Kimi Code first at 82.31 with ZCode at 69.53; fixing AutoDesign and Claude Code while varying the model ranks Claude 4.8 at 74.56 ahead of Seed 2.1 Pro at 71.83 and Kimi K2.7 at 70.12. One traced poster run shows the intended repair behavior concretely: an initial attempt scored 0.36 with a clipped analysis lane, reallocation of that row lifted it to 0.42 by the third attempt, a header refit and evidence scaling reached 0.62, and the ninth attempt preserved the repaired composition and was accepted at 0.78, with every edit confined to the flagged region while valid content carried forward. The headline autonomous-run statistic: 253 tool calls and 11 editing turns inside 40 minutes for under three dollars. The optimization trace itself ran seven days, invoking 224 subagents across at least 123 recursive iterations and accumulating 54 accepted-and-rejected harness updates, moving a representative paper from 49.00 initially to an 80.88 autonomous plateau, then 88.39 after humans redirected the search.

Skeptical read. The acceptance gate compares against training tasks evaluated by R meta, an evaluator built by another agent from annotated references, so evaluator drift is a live risk the authors acknowledge by flagging versioned evaluators with adversarial probes and periodic human audits as open problems. The seven-configuration ablation lives entirely on the ten-paper mini set, and gains there may not transfer proportionally to the hundred-paper track. Human study intervals are wide; 64 percent beats could plausibly be 55 percent. And the poster task has a specific structure, dense but bounded layouts with verifiable provenance, that flatters rule-based validation. Before adopting, verify the acceptance-gate signal survives swapping R meta for your own evaluator, check whether the one-component-per-iteration constraint still converges on your domain, and price the 17-to-85-minute runtimes against your actual use case. Bottom line: this is the most concrete demonstration yet that scaffold quality is a searchable, gated optimization surface rather than folklore, with a benchmark and ablation package solid enough to argue from. Treat the evaluator-in-the-loop construction as the main thing to re-verify before pointing it at your own production workflows.
