Coda-DaVinci, by Yiyan Ji, Qixun Wang, and Yang Shi, from a multi-institution collaboration spanning Nanjing University, Peking University, the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, the University of Hong Kong, and Tsinghua University, is a task-adaptive agentic framework for video generation that uses agent-authored 3D proxies, generated as code, to guide a video diffusion model. The paper has two parts. The first is a controlled study of how the Code2Video paradigm actually behaves, varying the granularity of the 3D proxy, whether physics simulation is applied, and how the prompt is aligned to the proxy, with blinded human evaluation on top of automated benchmarks. The second is the framework itself, which takes the findings from that study and turns them into a routing decision: for a given generation task, the system decides whether to use proxy-guided generation or to go directly to the video model, adapts the proxy granularity to each entity in the scene, and coordinates physics simulation with proxy-grounded prompting.

The prior-art context is the Code2Video paradigm, in which a coding agent writes a 3D proxy, a scene description in a 3D representation, that guides the video generator. The motivation is that explicit control over structure and dynamics, given by a 3D model, is harder to get from a pure text-to-video prompt, which is why people started adding a 3D code stage. But the paper's observation is that the community had not actually characterized this paradigm: how you construct the proxy, what granularity to use, whether to run a physics simulation, and how to write the prompt relative to the proxy were all open questions, and the capabilities and limits of the approach were underexplored. That is the gap the controlled study fills, and it is the more durable contribution, because it is a set of findings about when the paradigm works and when it does not.

The controlled study varies three factors. The first is proxy granularity, from coarse to fine, and the finding is that there is a task-dependent trade-off: coarse proxies favor natural motion, while medium-grained proxies favor scene consistency. That is a genuinely useful and somewhat counterintuitive result, because the naive expectation is that more detail in the proxy is always better, but the study finds that fine-grained proxies can constrain the motion in a way that makes it look less natural, while a coarser proxy leaves the generator more room to produce fluid dynamics at the cost of some consistency. The second factor is physics simulation, and the finding is that running a physics simulation on the proxy before generating improves average quality. The third is proxy-aligned prompting, where the text prompt is grounded in the specific proxy that was authored, and that also improves quality over a generic prompt. All three are incremental but compounding, and the point of the study is to give practitioners a decision rubric rather than a single fixed recipe.

Coda-DaVinci is the framework built on those findings. It is task-adaptive in a specific sense: rather than always using the proxy-guided path, it selects between proxy-guided generation and direct generation on a per-task basis, which is the routing decision the controlled study justifies. It adapts the modeling granularity to each entity in the scene, so different objects in the same scene can be represented at different levels of detail depending on what the task needs. It coordinates the physics simulation with proxy-grounded prompting, so the two quality improvements from the study are applied together rather than in isolation. The system is a multi-agent harness, and it has a repair loop: it preserves the scene assets across generations, and it guides repairs through visual inspection plus an independent review agent, so a failed generation is diagnosed and corrected rather than simply regenerated from scratch.

The evaluation uses a set of recent video benchmarks. R2M is used for scene consistency, VTR is used for physical behavior, and the paper reports leading instruction-following on VTR and leading scene consistency on R2M. It also reports improved physical plausibility over direct generation, and higher visual realism than the compared Code2Video baselines. Long-video evaluation is included, and the paper reports gains in event structure and in consistency across successive interactions, which is the regime where the proxy's persistent structure should matter most. The video synthesis backend across the benchmarks is a single commercial model, Seedance 2.5, with benchmark images supplied through a reference-media interface, so the results are about the agentic framing on top of a fixed generator, which is the right isolation for testing the framework's contribution but it also means the numbers are tied to that specific backend.

One detail from the controlled study is worth emphasizing because it changes how you would use the rubric. The granularity finding is not a single best setting, it is a trade-off that depends on what the task prioritizes, and the paper's own framing is that coarse proxies favor natural motion while medium-grained proxies favor consistency. That means the correct reading of the study is not "use a medium proxy" but "decide which of motion naturalness and scene consistency your task cares about more, and set the granularity accordingly." The physics simulation and proxy-aligned prompting findings are less conditional, they improve average quality across the board, so those two are the safe defaults, while the granularity is the task-specific dial. The framework's per-entity granularity adaptation is the mechanism that lets a single scene use different granularities for different objects, which is a reasonable response to the finding, because in a busy scene the foreground object might need a finer proxy for consistency while a background element can stay coarse to keep its motion natural.

Now the skeptical reading. The most important caveat is that the entire system is evaluated on top of one video generator, Seedance 2.5, and the gains are about the agentic routing and the proxy construction, not about the underlying model. If you swap the backend, the routing decisions that look optimal on Seedance 2.5 may not be optimal on a different generator, because the trade-off between proxy granularity and motion naturalness is a property of the joint system, not of the proxy alone. That means the controlled study's findings, while valuable, are backend-specific in a way the paper does not fully stress, and you would want to re-run at least the granularity trade-off on your own generator before treating the rubric as universal.

The second caveat is the task-adaptive routing. The framework decides per task whether to use the proxy path, and that decision is the thing that differentiates it from a fixed pipeline, but the paper does not give a detailed breakdown of how often the router chooses each path and how sensitive the overall quality is to a misroute. A routing error, sending a task down the proxy path when direct generation would have been better, or the reverse, is a failure mode that a per-task router is structurally exposed to, and without an ablation on routing accuracy it is hard to know how much of the reported gain is from good routing versus from the proxy and simulation improvements that would apply even with a fixed policy.

For implementation, the transferable assets are the controlled study's rubric, the granularity trade-off finding, and the repair loop with independent review. The granularity finding is the one I would take away first, because it reframes proxy construction from "make it as detailed as possible" to "match the granularity to whether the task cares more about natural motion or about consistency." The repair loop is the second, because an independent review agent that inspects the generated video and triggers a targeted repair is a pattern you can drop into any generative pipeline, not just a Code2Video one. The thing to verify before adopting the full framework is the router, since that is the component most likely to not transfer across backends.

To summarize the bottom line. Coda-DaVinci is a well-structured contribution whose real value is the controlled study of the Code2Video paradigm, the finding that proxy granularity has a task-dependent trade-off between natural motion and consistency, plus a task-adaptive agentic framework that routes between proxy-guided and direct generation and repairs through independent review. Its limits are that everything is measured on a single video backend, so the routing rubric is not portable for free, and the per-task router is the least characterized component, so re-run the granularity trade-off on your own generator and ablate the routing before trusting the adaptive claims.
