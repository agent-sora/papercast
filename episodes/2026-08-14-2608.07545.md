DarwinX: Evolving Agent Harnesses Through Natural Selection, by Yifan Zhang, Yutong Dai, Juntao Tan, and colleagues at Salesforce AI Research and Salesforce Agentforce. The premise: an agent's capability lives partly in its harness, the prompts, tools, skills, memory, and control flow wrapped around the model, and that harness can be improved by selection over a population rather than by editing a single lineage. The underlying model, Salesforce's proprietary Monet agent, stays frozen in every experiment, so every reported delta is attributable to the harness alone. Fitness comes exclusively from each benchmark's own verifier: no gold solutions, no hand-picked winners.

The motivation comes from two documented failure modes in self-improving agents. First, path dependence: single-lineage self-editors such as SICA plateau because early edits bias everything downstream. Second, cross-task interference: an edit that fixes one family of tasks silently regresses another, which stalls evolution over mixed task distributions. The Darwin Godel Machine, the closest prior system, mutates one parent at a time and scores the child against that parent, so lineages that solve complementary tasks are never brought back together, and a gain carries no obligation to hold what it displaces. HarnessX isolates task families to contain interference but leaves the resulting specialists in separate lineages. DarwinX keeps the common inner loop, batch rollouts, reflect, propose a bounded edit, gate on regression, and changes the selection wrapped around it.

Mechanically, the archive is a tree whose nodes are harness snapshots with edit deltas, per-task scores, trial evidence, and distilled lessons. The harness spans two editable layers: skill (prompts, memory, distilled knowledge) and code (tools, control flow, the agent loop). Each variant is scored by per-task solve rate, avg@k. For a child against its parent, the system computes a net gain and a bounded regression term, and admits the child only when net gain is positive and regression stays within a tolerance. Promotion is deliberately two-speed: a reasoned verifier agent can admit a child on promising but noisy evidence so the search keeps moving, but a promoted child must pass stricter avg@k re-testing plus a preservation probe that re-samples the lineage's known solved set before it may steer future search. Parent selection ranks nodes by cumulative lineage gain, which matters because variants are screened on different task subsets, and samples from the highest-gain node with probability one minus beta, otherwise broadening across the wider population. The gate does real work in practice: over the run it accepted 26 iterations and reverted 36, and accepted checkpoints score materially higher than rejected ones.

Three learning signals share one edit interface, and none touch model weights. Failure-derived signals summarize failed trajectories and localize missing capabilities; teacher-derived signals distill a stronger solver's successful rollout into a reusable approach and are reserved for walls, tasks where no agent rollout succeeds; self-derived signals contrast the agent's own passing and failing rollouts on variance-band tasks where both exist. Parallel branches target different capability clusters, on Terminal-Bench 2.1: numerical ML, low-level systems, bio and assembly, parsing and text tools, database tasks. Variants that preserve every inherited solve stay eligible for inheritance; those that trade solves contribute distilled lessons only. When complementary specialists exist, DarwinX merges their additive edits from a common ancestor and keeps the merged child only if it covers the union of its parents' solved sets, so a merge can only add coverage.

Results follow a ladder of increasing separation between the evolution signal and the test. In-domain, on Terminal-Bench 2.1's 89 tasks with official avg@5 reporting: base Monet on frozen GPT-5.5 sits at 75.5 percent; DarwinX lifts it to 83.2, level with Codex plus GPT-5.5 at 83.1 and above Terminus-2 at 78.0. On GPT-5.6 Sol at medium effort it reaches 84.7 plus or minus 1.2, matching or exceeding the verified leaderboard leader, Claude Code with Fable 5 at 83.8 running at xhigh effort. Gains concentrate where the frozen base has headroom: ML and scientific computing rise from 60.1 to 74.9 percent, data and database tasks from 83.9 to 97.8, while already-strong clusters move within noise. Of 88 paired tasks, 36 improve, 43 are unchanged, 9 regress. The compute objection gets a direct answer: on the six tasks that flip from failing to solved, the evolved harness spends roughly double the turns, 22 versus 11, and quadruple the tokens, about 380,000 versus 89,000, while compute on the 69 already-solved tasks barely moves. The submission audit found no harness-level gaming: of 370 rewarded trajectories only two were flagged, and the one confirmed shortcut, an agent reading an answer-bearing string from a task's own published README on a single trial, is a per-trial policy lapse; the harness solves the same task legitimately in three of the other four samples.

Held-out generalization uses TerminalWorld: evolve on 94 training tasks, freeze, evaluate single-attempt on 41 disjoint tasks. Training-subset score saturates from 0.505 to 1.000, yet held-out pass@1 is 68.3 percent on Opus 4.8, a 31.7-point gap between proxy and truth, and the best result on the split, above Claude Code at 65.9 and base Monet at 61.0. Four high-scoring specialists solve 24 to 27 held-out tasks each; the merged harness reaches 28, beating every specialist, up from 25 for the unevolved base, with the matched GPT-5.5 pair moving from 20 to 23, which is the clearest evidence that keeping an archive beats greedily following the in-loop score. Caveats are stated plainly: the same procedure on GPT-5.5 reaches only 56.1, below Terminus-2, and with 41 tasks one solve moves the metric by 2.4 points; the one-task margin over Claude Code carries a paired McNemar p-value of 1.0.

Synthetic-to-real is the widest jump: evolve a browser harness on 300 synthetic intents generated from application descriptions, judged by an LLM at avg@3 and avg@5, then evaluate deterministically on 1,260 real WebArena-Infinity tasks. The harness exposes Chrome through the DevTools Protocol and lets the agent write JavaScript to observe and control pages, which makes auditing essential. Audit-clean pass@1, counting flagged trajectories as failures, goes from 43.5 percent for base Monet to 93.0. Invalid trajectories fall from 293 to 17: evaluation-plane access violations drop from 155 to zero, privileged-host access from 97 to zero, exploit-class behavior from 26 to zero, leaving only raw-state mutations concentrated in one application. Confirmed-invalid rate falls from 23.5 to 1.4 percent. The evolved harness adds four contract-oriented skills: derive an acceptance contract, inspect client-visible state, prefer app-owned semantic operations, verify persistence. Cross-benchmark transfer is thinner: the Terminal-Bench harness, run unchanged on all 500 SWE-bench Verified issues on frozen Opus 4.8, reaches 84.2 percent official pass@1 against an 80.8 percent fix-skill reference, a real but narrow 3.4-point edge with no in-domain feedback. The ablation finds all seven added skills sit in one verification and artifact-contract family, offered as plausible attribution, not causal isolation.

Skeptical read: the matched-model deltas on Terminal-Bench and WebArena-Infinity are the load-bearing results, and the validity audit showing compliance improving alongside capability is the most interesting finding here. But the experiments evaluate the whole system, archive, selector, merge operator, and inference effort together, with no randomized operator ablations; recombination's contribution is asserted from one benchmark; the TerminalWorld margin is statistically fragile; and the frontier rows are self-reported submissions. Before adopting, re-run the preservation probe and the validity audit independently, since both are the mechanisms doing the quiet work. Bottom line: this is the strongest current evidence that population-level selection over harnesses converts evaluation compute into general agent capability on a frozen model. Treat the per-skill attribution story as unproven and the headline gains, especially the 49.5-point WebArena jump, as system-level claims worth replicating before you cite them.
