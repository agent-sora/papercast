LociArt, by Yuxuan Chen, Feng Xie, and Jiagao Hu, from MiLM Plus at Xiaomi and Xi'an University of Electronic Science and Technology, is a video editing method that treats local stylization as a first-class problem instead of a special case of global style transfer. The task they define is free-style local video stylization. You specify where to stylize, with a mask that propagates across frames, and you specify what style to apply, and the model has to restyle exactly that region while preserving everything else, and it has to hold up across time. The hard part is not the style, there are plenty of good stylizers. The hard part is the association, binding a particular style to a particular moving region without leaking it into the background, and keeping the boundary clean as the subject moves.

The model is called GRNStyle, and it builds on Generative Refinement Networks, which are iterative refinement models for editing rather than one-shot diffusion. GRN models refine an output over multiple steps, which suits a local edit well, because preservation is exactly the kind of constraint that is easier to enforce iteratively, keeping the untouched regions close to the source at each refinement step, than in a single denoising pass. The source-evidence editing formulation from GRNEdit is the starting point, where the unedited video serves as evidence for what should not change, and the core design is a polar style-aware mask. Instead of encoding the style as a scalar or an index that imposes an ordering on styles, the mask embeds style identities as equal-norm directions in a learned space, one direction per style, within the selected region. So the where and the what are unified in a single conditioning signal, the mask says which pixels to touch and which style direction to apply, and because the style is a direction rather than a rank, you can compose multiple regions with multiple styles without the model needing a notion of which style comes first. The complementary piece is representation-geometry conditioning, and the motivation for it is a specific failure mode. If you pass the mask through the VAE to encode it, you get learned spatiotemporal features that carry context, but the VAE compression blurs the mask geometry, and the edit leaks past the boundary. If you resample the raw mask directly, you keep the exact spatial support but lose the learned temporal context. So they use both, VAE-encoded mask features for the spatiotemporal understanding and the raw mask as geometric support for the localization, and the two together give the model both the temporal understanding it needs to propagate the edit and the spatial precision it needs to localize it. Source evidence from the unedited video guides content preservation throughout the refinement, so the model knows what it is supposed to keep.

The data story is the part that makes the whole thing reproducible. Training pairs for local stylization do not exist in the wild, because nobody films the same scene twice, once normal and once stylized in just the foreground. The existing data sources do not fit either, global stylization pairs stylize the whole frame and give no supervision on preservation, and instruction-based video editing datasets cover broad operations that are not local restyling. So they build them automatically. The pipeline takes a source video, applies global stylization to get a fully styled version, runs video object removal to get clean background content, estimates alpha mattes for both the original and the stylized objects, and composites the pieces back together so the target video has the stylized region and the untouched background, with the boundary handled along the matte contour rather than a hard mask edge. That pipeline produces the LociArt 100K dataset, one hundred thousand local stylization pairs, each with a source video, an editing mask, a style instruction, and a locally stylized target, supervising both the transformation and the preservation at the same time. The fact that the targets are composited rather than organically generated is the main caveat, and it is worth knowing before you trust the preservation metrics, because a composite is the best case for a clean boundary.

Evaluation is the second contribution, because existing metrics for video stylization average over the whole frame and will happily report a good score when the style is only weakly present in the region you asked for. The LociArt Benchmark has eighty source videos and eighty four object-mask units, each evaluated under both foreground and background stylization, giving one hundred sixty eight cases. The central metric is Style Prototype Similarity, or SPS, which measures regional style fidelity by comparing the stylized region against a prototype of the target style, so a model that leaks the style into the background or under-applies it in the region scores lower even if the global average looks fine. They pair it with measures of non-target preservation and temporal consistency, because the three failure modes of local editing, weak regional style, changes outside the target, and flicker over time, need separate scores.

On the results, in the human evaluation with one hundred sixty ratings on a zero to five scale, GRNStyle scores four point three four on style fidelity and four point four seven on background preservation, against two point five two and three point one four for CoinVE-Edit and three point eight six for Seedance on the preservation side. The per-style breakdown shows gains over the strongest baseline in most cases, with the largest gains in pencil sketch, oil painting, and claymation, and the ablation on the mask conditioning shows that the representation-geometry combination is what improves spatial localization, with the raw geometric support contributing most on the preservation side. The zero-shot result is the headline. The model is trained only on single-region, single-style pairs, and it generalizes to compositions it has never seen, two subjects with two different styles in one frame, or the same style applied to multiple masks, which is a genuinely useful property for an editing tool, because artists do not want to retrain or re-prompt for every combination.

What I would trust is the formulation and the benchmark, because region-aware evaluation is a real gap and the SPS metric is a reasonable first cut at closing it. The three failure modes they separate, weak regional style, changes outside the target region, and flicker over time, are exactly the failure modes that a video-averaged FID or CLIP score will miss, and having separate scores for each is what makes the benchmark usable for development instead of just for a paper. What I would re-verify before adopting GRNStyle in a product is the preservation quality on real footage, since the training targets are composited and the boundary handling is exactly the part a composite makes look better than organic data would, and the temporal consistency on long, fast-motion clips, which is where refinement models tend to start flickering. The zero-shot composition results are the part I would test first, because the multi-region, multi-style capability is the feature an artist would actually pay for, and it is the capability most likely to degrade in practice, since the training pairs never show two styles at once and the model has to extrapolate the composition from the equal-norm style directions. The engineering takeaway is the data pipeline, because if you want to train your own local editor, the stylize, remove, matte, and composite loop is the template, and the polar mask encoding is the clean way to make region and style a single conditioning signal. LociArt is a solid method paper with a useful dataset and a better evaluation scheme, and the numbers it reports, four point three four on style fidelity and four point four seven on preservation against the closest baselines at two point five two and three point one four, are consistent with the qualitative claim that the region-style association is the thing they actually fixed.
