## FM-Bench: Twenty Years in the Manager's Chair

First, FM-Bench, a benchmark for long-horizon decision-making with competing agents. An LLM runs a football club for twenty in-game years through twenty-six tools and roughly three to four hundred decision stops: drafting a squad on the same budget as every rival, trading players, negotiating contracts, investing in facilities and youth — while a board can fire it, and a deterministic engine accumulates everything into one final score. No LLM judge, no human rater.

Fifteen frontier models completed every horizon in the solo track while blind scripted baselines died out in most of theirs. Claude Fable Five topped the solo board, but in the shared twenty-year Arena the title rotated among ten different models — neither scale, price, nor vendor predicted the order, and the order settled only late in the horizon. What separated winners was managerial behaviour: they cut slow-payoff investment near the end, kept cash invested rather than idle, and opened contract renewals well before deadlines. Token spend predicted nothing. Self-managed memory failed in two opposite modes — an archive that only grows, or a plan rewritten every season. For anyone evaluating agents beyond single tasks, this is what long-horizon measurement looks like.

## Co-RL: Reasoning Without Rewards

Second, Co-RL. Reinforcement learning for reasoning usually leans on verifiable rewards — ground-truth answers that get scarcer as capability advances. Pure self-rewarding alternatives tend to reinforce their own biases until responses homogenize and training collapses. Co-RL's move is cooperative: several parameter-independent models are trained simultaneously, each deriving reward signals from its peers. Because the models err differently, cohort diversity — heterogeneous families, sizes, even rephrased prompts — breaks the correlated-error loop that kills self-rewarding training.

Across seven text-only benchmarks, gains of three to eight point six percent for language models, and up to seven percent on multimodal suites — with no ground-truth labels anywhere, matching or beating supervised methods. If label scarcity is your bottleneck, peer-derived reward among a diverse cohort looks like the most credible label-free recipe yet.

## SkillGate: The Credit Starvation Bug in Skill Selection

Third, SkillGate, which finds and fixes a structural bug in training agents that read skills mid-episode. Public libraries hold thousands of skill files; choosing which to load is a decision the policy makes during the task. But train that choice with ordinary outcome-rewarded RL and you hit what the authors name selector credit starvation: under sequence-level advantage, the few tokens naming the chosen skill carry a vanishing share of the loss, and their inherited credit turns wrong-signed as trajectories lengthen. A correct choice gets punished whenever later execution fails.

SkillGate partitions credit by construction: outcome credit reaches only execution tokens, while a separate action-local advantage reaches exactly the skill-naming tokens. On five agentic benchmarks with sixteen-candidate slates, a nine-billion-parameter policy climbs from forty-one to fifty-three percent success — ahead of identical budgets spent on plain outcome reward — while exposing itself to misleading skills far less often. The general lesson: when a trajectory mixes one-shot choices with long execution, give them separate credit channels or the choice learns nothing.

## Bounded Agents: Authorization for Multi-Agent Systems

Fourth, Bounded Agents, on delegation security. Agent permissions today are set once at session start and evaluated request-by-request — so an agent may act contrary to its task, combine individually permitted actions into a prohibited outcome, or delegate authority downward without limits. Prompt injection is only dangerous because the agent has authority; this is an authorization architecture problem, not just a model problem.

The Agentic Principal Chain tracks delegated authority from principal to principal, evaluates each request against accumulated session state with six checks, carries scope and budget restrictions into sub-agents, uses composition closure to block prohibited combinations, and enforces decisions entirely outside the model. Results: AgentDojo exfiltration fell from seventy-five to a hundred percent down to zero across all domains; all five hundred forty-four InjecAgent data-stealing cases blocked; destruction and manipulation dropped by an order of magnitude — at zero point two four milliseconds ninetieth-ninth-percentile latency, for a utility cost of about nine to fourteen points. With proofs of blast-radius monotonicity attached. If your agents touch cloud services, this is the paper to copy from.

## Training Leaves Traces

Fifth, "Training Leaves Traces." Can weights alone reveal whether two checkpoints share ancestry — with no data, no logs, just the tensors? The trick: strip away the identity-aligned component that any shared training produces, then compare checkpoint-specific structure across residual blocks for a calibrated lineage score. On GPT-2 style benchmarks it separates fine-tuned, LoRA-merged, pruned, and quantized descendants from independent and distilled look-alikes with area under the curve of one point zero, survives function-preserving laundering attempts that defeat weight-space baselines, runs seventy-six times faster than the nearest robust competitor, and correctly sorted ten public LLaMA-Two checkpoints into three related and seven unrelated.

For supply-chain security and model provenance, this establishes a passive signal: your fine-tunes carry a family resemblance that anyone with the weights can read. Assume merges are not anonymous.

## MuseCPEval: Does Your Edit Preserve the Music?

Last today, MuseCPEval — for the music technologists. Modern systems do timbre transfer, instrument substitution, genre transformation; almost none rigorously measure whether facets that should stay unchanged actually do. That property is Music Context Preservation, and MuseCPEval is its first dedicated evaluation framework: four categories of musical facets, fine-grained metrics for nuanced attribute changes, objective validation plus a human study, and case studies that expose where current editing systems leak the very things they were supposed to keep.

Evaluation papers rarely excite, but this addresses the core failure mode of generative audio editing: unrequested drift. Whatever you generate — audio, images, documents — the discipline of measuring what must not change is the difference between an edit and a regeneration.
