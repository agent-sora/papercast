OmniEdu, from Hao Liang, Qihan Lin, and Meiyi Qiang of Peking University with colleagues at the University of the Chinese Academy of Sciences and Zhongguancun Academy, is a new open model family, and this is the episode where the standing rule matters: any paper that introduces a new foundation model gets covered, and OmniEdu qualifies. It is a family of open K-12 foundation models at three sizes, 4B, 9B, and 27B, fine-tuned from Qwen 3.5 base models with a capability-oriented instruction-tuning corpus, and the contribution is the data pipeline and the capability taxonomy as much as the weights. The 27B model reaches 63.12 percent exact match and 76.69 percent F1 on K12-Bench, 85.89 percent on MathFish, 86.95 percent on EDUMATH, 78.74 percent on the scaffold setting of MathTutorBench, and the best teaching average of 3.02 on LongTutor among the models they evaluate.

The framing is a taxonomy. Educational language models tend to specialize: some solve problems well, some can tutor, but training mixtures are usually organized by source or by task, and the four capabilities a useful educational system needs get muddled together. OmniEdu organizes supervision around four explicit capabilities. Subject competence, actually solving K-12 problems in mathematics, science, and reading. Curriculum grounding, understanding how knowledge is structured in official textbooks and standards, which is what lets a model say this topic comes after that one in the grade level. Diagnostic reasoning, recognizing where a student is actually failing rather than just grading the answer. And pedagogical action and scaffolding, producing the instructional response, the hint sequence, the adaptive explanation, that matches the student's state. The pipeline that builds the corpus is where the engineering lives: it starts from more than 100 educational and general instruction datasets, an initial pool of roughly 1.34 million examples, runs deterministic cleaning, then semantic auditing and rewriting, where a 122B model performs quality control that rules cannot, then task-specific quality scoring, token-budgeted diversity selection, and finally pedagogical instruction assignment. The output is 69,999 examples and 15.96 million supervised response tokens, of which 60,951 are education-specific and about 9,000 are general instruction-following data held in the mixture on purpose, so the models do not lose their base capabilities. Two design choices stand out. First, the pedagogical capability is taught through instruction assignment: every example gets exactly one system instruction that names the desired behavior, answer with justification, scaffold, give feedback, or adapt the explanation to the student, while the original content stays untouched, which lets a single model support answer-oriented and scaffold-oriented interactions and makes the behavior explicit in the input. Second, the mixture is capability-weighted rather than source-weighted, with pedagogical action and scaffolding at about 20 percent of the education-specific examples, curriculum grounding at about 13 percent, drawn from sources like the K12 knowledge graph, TutorBench, MathTutor, and the LongTutor synthetic, silver, and gold tiers.

The training is deliberately plain: LlamaFactory, a learning rate of 5 times 10 to the minus 6, 32K sequence length, three epochs, applied to three base models, Qwen 3.5 4B base, Qwen 3.5 9B base, and Qwen 3.8 27B. There is no preference optimization or RL in the recipe, which is itself a finding: the gains come from curation, not from exotic objectives. The evaluation is built to match the taxonomy, which is both the strength and the weakness. Curriculum grounding is tested on K12-Bench, MathFish, and EDUMATH, where EDUMATH builds targeted supervision from a knowledge graph extracted from official K-12 textbooks. Authentic problem solving, the harder test, uses GAOKAO-Bench, the Chinese college entrance exam benchmark, EXAMS-V, and MDK12-Bench, which are real school-level assessments rather than synthetic questions. Tutoring is measured on MathTutorBench, which tests scaffolding, feedback, and adaptive explanation, TutorBench, and LongTutor, which adds longitudinal student modeling, tracking a student across a sequence of interactions. The tutoring results are where the corpus design pays off most dramatically. On MathTutorBench, the scaffold win rate jumps from 57.16 to 78.74 percent for the 27B model, and the smaller models gain even more, with the 4B improving by 55.4 points and the 9B by 61.3 points over their bases, from near-chance starting points. On LongTutor, the evidence average, which measures whether the tutor actually uses the student's learning history, rises from 25.67 to 65.88 percent for the 4B, from 5.81 to 66.63 for the 9B, and from 36.80 to 78.20 for the 27B, while Claude Opus 5 scores higher on evidence but the 27B takes the best teaching average of 3.02. The baselines are both the open-weight educational family, Confucius 3 Math, MuduoLLM, and the EduChat variants, spanning domain pretraining, supervised fine-tuning, preference optimization, and reinforcement learning, and the frontier proprietary models, GPT 5.4, GPT 5.6 Sol, Claude Opus 5, GLM 5.3, and Kimi K3. The central result, across all three model scales, is that education-oriented tuning improves all three capability groups relative to the corresponding base model, and they are careful to compare every fine-tuned model against its own base to isolate the data's effect from the model's.

The skeptical read. The concrete numbers make the pattern clearer than any average. On K12-Bench, OmniEdu-27B lifts exact match from 52.11 to 63.12 percent and F1 from 73.48 to 76.69 percent over its Qwen 3.8 27B base, and the smaller models move too, with the 4B gaining 11.5 exact-match points and the 9B gaining 6.9. On EDUMATH, the gains are the largest in the paper: the 4B, 9B, and 27B models improve their MaC scores by 20.8, 15.4, and 16.35 points respectively, which is the cleanest evidence that textbook knowledge-graph grounding transfers across scales. On the authentic exam benchmarks, OmniEdu-27B raises the GAOKAO-Bench full-score rate from 91.55 to 94.87 percent, and the 4B posts the single most striking jump in the study, its EXAMS-V accuracy rising from 44.69 to 57.62 percent. On MDK12-Bench, the 27B moves the overall score from 46.04 to 57.76 percent, with open-question correctness rising from 62.08 to 78.77 percent, which is the number that matters most for a tutoring deployment, since open questions are where a model either explains or merely regurgitates. The open-weight educational baselines mostly look weak by comparison, with Confucius 3 Math scoring 5.23 exact match on K12-Bench, while the frontier proprietary models keep an advantage on some exam-style evaluations, which the paper concedes rather than papering over. The scale is modest, a 27B maximum in a world where educational use cases increasingly reach for frontier models, and the family's value is in the open weights and the reproducible pipeline rather than raw capability. The benchmark suite is heavily Chinese K-12 in flavor, GAOKAO-Bench and textbook graphs from Chinese curricula, so the cross-curriculum transfer of the curriculum-grounding capability is not really tested. The tutoring numbers, particularly the 3.02 teaching average on LongTutor, are relative to a set of evaluated models, not a guarantee against the best proprietary tutors. And the 70K example corpus is small enough that the results say a lot about curation quality and a fair amount less about scale.

Bottom line: OmniEdu is the kind of release that matters less for leaderboard position and more for method: a clean demonstration that a capability taxonomy, an auditing pipeline with a large model as judge, and token-budgeted diversity selection can turn base open models into a coherent educational family, with the weights, the dataset, and the code all public. It also makes the strongest case yet that scaffolding ability, the thing most educational deployments actually need, is learnable from curated data alone. If you are building educational tooling on open models, the data recipe is the artifact to study, and the 27B checkpoint is a reasonable open starting point.
