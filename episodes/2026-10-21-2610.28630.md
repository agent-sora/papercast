The paper is SolarRenderer, from Guian Fang, Shengju Qian, and Dongyue Lu, at the National University of Singapore, with Lightspeed, CUHK Shenzhen, Shanghai AI Laboratory, and HKUST Guangzhou, led by Yiren Song and Mike Zheng Shou. It tackles a specific rendering problem: game engines and coding agents can specify the layout, motion, and camera of a scene, but turning that coarse structure into realistic video needs a renderer that follows the structure and fills in the appearance it leaves open. Existing renderers are tied to the representation of one engine, need tens of denoising steps, and train on conditions that are not frame-aligned with their target videos. SolarRenderer adapts a frozen thirty-three billion MiniMax-H3 backbone with two lightweight LoRAs, renders in four steps, which is twelve point three times faster than fifty-step sampling, and generalizes to structural representations it has never seen in training.

The first problem is that control is tied to a fixed representation. Engine-driven renderers are built around the one thing their engine exports, white-box frames, semantic maps, proxy videos, or full G-buffers, and general controllable generators either attach a branch per condition or label the type of each input, so a new engine or a new proxy kind calls for a new model. The second problem is speed, because a large video backbone needs tens of denoising steps and distilling a structure-conditioned model from scratch at this scale is costly. The third problem is that training pairs are rarely aligned frame by frame, because conditions estimated from real videos carry the errors of the estimator and differ from the engine proxies the renderer gets at test time.

The design answer to the first problem is to feed structural video through the native reference pathway of the backbone rather than attaching a ControlNet branch or concatenating channels, both of which add parameters trained from scratch. The MiniMax-H3 backbone already packs text, reference videos, audio, and the target video into one full-attention sequence, so the control adapter can inherit the pretrained ability to read reference videos. But there is a subtlety: native reference tokens occupy a separate temporal segment before the clip with their own spatial grid, so a zero-shot backbone uses a structural video as a loose reference and copies it or changes the camera instead of following the structure. The fix is frame-aligned positional encoding. Rotary position embedding makes attention depend on the relative offset between tokens, so they give each reference token the RoPE coordinates of the target token it describes, meaning the k-th structural latent frame shares the temporal index and spatial grid of the k-th target frame, and the optional first frame is encoded as a static clip at index zero. The tokens at the same position stay distinguishable through their timesteps, because reference tokens keep the near-clean value the backbone assigns to reference inputs while target tokens carry the current noise level. The references are also kept untyped, with no type identifier or modality embedding, so the adapter infers what a reference constrains from the structural video itself, and the prompt describes each representation with two fixed sentences, a definition and a rule that names the attributes to follow and the visual properties that must not appear. That means a new format needs only a new description, no added parameters or retraining.

The training is deliberately decoupled into two stages on one frozen backbone, because training control and acceleration together does not work, whenever the acceleration objective can update the control adapter it also alters how the structure is followed. In stage one, they train a control LoRA with the standard flow-matching objective, a low-rank update added to the frozen attention and feed-forward projections of every block, with the loss computed on the target video tokens only. In stage two, they start from the rank-128 four-step LoRA of DMAD, which predicts a clean sample at every step and re-noises it for the next, initialize the first 128 ranks of a rank-384 LoRA so training starts from that update with room to grow, and update only the acceleration adapter while keeping the control LoRA frozen, so the control is preserved. The trick is structure-guided distribution matching, a modification of distribution matching distillation where the teacher is extrapolated along the structural condition. Because full-step sampling of the backbone is blurry and the composed student is already sharper than its teacher, plain distribution matching would pull it back toward that blur. So they amplify the structural signal of the teacher by taking the teacher's clean prediction with the structure and subtracting its prediction after the structural tokens are removed, isolating what the structural video contributes such as contours and character positions, and pushing the student toward a target that is sharper and more decisive than the teacher itself, with an extrapolation weight of 1.5. This is essentially classifier-free guidance applied to the structural condition alone.

For streaming, a block-causal variant extends the same two-stage design, splitting the clip into blocks of at most five latent frames, adapting the control LoRA with teacher forcing so each block attends to the prompt, the first frame, its own structure, and the five preceding blocks but never a later block, and then post-training the acceleration LoRA on the student's own block-by-block rollouts. At inference the acceleration LoRA is merged into the backbone, each block is sampled in four steps, and with a streaming VAE decoder the model renders 768p video in real time at up to twenty-eight frames per second.

The data is a frame-aligned corpus of 136K clips, about 243 hours, from eight sources, game engines, simulators, and YouTube videos, where the structure is either exported by an engine or fitted to the video, and every structural frame is checked to match its target in content and timing. The corpus mixes conditions, with the largest sources being human-played Unreal gameplay with grey block-outs and urban driving with semantic segmentation, alongside depth and object-mask plus skeleton conditions and fitted body meshes from real video. That frame alignment is what the authors argue the prior work got wrong, because a renderer that has been trained on misaligned pairs will learn to ignore the structure it is supposed to follow.

The results are strong on their own terms. On an in-distribution test set and a generalization benchmark covering sixteen structural representations, eleven of them unseen in training, the four-step model outperforms existing acceleration LoRAs and the official backbone, surpasses existing structure-conditioned renderers, and achieves a new state of the art in structure-conditioned rendering. The generalization is the headline: it faithfully renders pose skeletons, pencil storyboards, children's crayon drawings, wireframes, and point clouds, none of which appears in training, and the ablations show the single control LoRA is ahead of the prior plug-in on every category, seen or unseen. The post-training ablations matter too, structure-guided post-training lifts the DMAD starting point, and pushing the extrapolation weight to 2 actually lowers the rating, so the guidance strength is a real knob.

Where I would push back. The generalization to unseen structure is impressive, but the benchmark is their own, sixteen representations they selected, and the Elo ratings are Gemini-based, so the magnitude of the state-of-the-art claim rests on a self-constructed evaluation and a single judge. The four-step speedup is relative to a fifty-step baseline that is arguably already a generous reference, and the real-time causal variant is a separate model with its own training, not the same weights. The frozen-backbone design is a strength for portability but a ceiling, because the appearance prior comes entirely from the pretrained model, and the structural control is only as good as the reference pathway's ability to interpret an untyped video it has never seen in that form. The engineering gotchas: the frame-aligned positional encoding has to be set up exactly, or the backbone falls back to copying the reference, and the decoupled two-stage order is load-bearing, because distilling before the control adapter is stable will not reproduce the numbers. It is a genuinely clean, well-motivated contribution to structure-conditioned video rendering, and the untyped-reference-plus-frame-aligned-positioning idea is the part worth stealing for any controllable video model you build.
