VisionWeave: Weaving Elastic Visual Representations as a Native Capability of MLLMs, from Yuan Feng, Qize Yang, and Ruizhe Chen at the University of Science and Technology of China, Alibaba Token Hub at Alibaba Group, the Chinese University of Hong Kong, and Zhejiang University. The problem is a cost problem inside multimodal large language models. MLLMs encode their visual input into dense, fixed-size patch tokens, so a high-resolution image or a long video produces a wall of visual tokens regardless of how much of the image actually carries information. But visual information is unevenly distributed. Some regions need fine-grained detail, and other regions admit a compact representation. Downsampling the whole image sacrifices the detail where it matters. Existing token-pruning and adaptive approaches are limited in how content-adaptive they are, in how well they generalize across tasks, and in how deeply they are integrated into modern MLLMs and serving stacks. The paper's idea is to make the visual representation elastic and native: the model itself decides, per region, how much representational capacity to spend, so the token count adapts to the content instead of to a fixed budget.
The method has two components. A gated spatial pooler constructs coarse-grained representations alongside the model's native fine-grained patch representations, and it does so within a shared MRoPE coordinate, so the coarse tokens and the fine tokens live in the same positional space and the LLM does not have to learn a new geometry. A granularity router then learns a content-adaptive allocation, deciding per spatial block whether that region is better served by its fine-grained tokens or by a compact coarse representation. The inference procedure is a single vision transformer forward pass that reads the features at three depths, zero, half, and the final layer, and then a local cross-attention loop fuses those multi-scale features into per-block representations, which the router scores against a threshold. The default threshold is zero point five, which is equivalent to selecting the granularity with the higher predicted probability. Training is done with self-distillation only, no external teacher, where the native fine-grained forward pass supplies the output-distribution supervision and the distilled student learns to route. They validate the capability on the small Qwen3.5-4B model first, then scale it to Qwen3.8-27B with over thirty thousand A100 GPU-hours. The headline efficiency-quality tradeoff is the reason the paper exists: based on Qwen3.8-27B, VisionWeave adaptively saves forty three percent of its visual tokens on average while retaining ninety eight point nine percent of native performance across eight benchmarks, whereas the token-pruning baselines, forced to a fixed fifty percent savings target, preserve only eighty eight percent of native performance.
Two properties make this practical rather than a research curiosity. First, the token savings are genuinely content-adaptive. The savings differ substantially by input: on real-world visual tokens they go from one thousand seven hundred twenty-eight down to eight hundred sixty-seven, about a fifty percent cut; on chart tokens from one thousand nine hundred to one thousand fifty-four, about forty four percent; on driving scenes from one thousand three hundred eighty to eight hundred forty. The savings are not a fixed dial, they track the information in the image. Second, and this is the key selling point for a serving team, the same threshold that sets the quality-cost tradeoff is tunable at inference time without retraining. On Video-MME, lowering the threshold from zero point five to zero point two five increases the token savings on the twenty-seven-billion model from fifty four point two percent to sixty six percent, for a performance loss of only zero point three seven points. At the full-retention endpoint the model behaves exactly like the native model with no savings, so one deployed model spans the whole efficiency-quality curve. The serving numbers, on SGLang with two A100 eighty-gigabyte GPUs, are where the paper earns its keep for an inference team: VisionWeave lifts end-to-end throughput from zero point nine to two point zero seven requests per minute, a two point three times speedup, and cuts mean time to first token from one hundred sixty nine point nine seconds to seventy seven point five, a fifty four point four percent reduction, with mean time per output token dropping from three hundred fifty five point six to one hundred forty point two nine milliseconds, a sixty point six percent reduction. The ablations back the design choices: the gated spatial pooler beats a pixel-unshuffle projector across all eight benchmarks, and when they shuffle the router's block-wise scores to break the alignment between routing and content, mean scores drop by two point three one points on the four-billion model and five point three six points on the twenty-seven-billion model, which is direct evidence that the routing is doing content-dependent work rather than just compressing uniformly. The training side is worth stating precisely, because it is what makes the method cheap to add to an existing model. VisionWeave is trained with self-distillation only, which means the teacher is the model's own native fine-grained forward pass. The native pass, where every block uses its fine-grained tokens, supplies the output-distribution supervision, and the distilled student learns to route each block to either its fine-grained or its coarse representation so as to match that distribution. There is no external teacher, no extra model to serve, and no change to the LLM weights, which is why the whole thing is a vision-side addition rather than a retraining of the language model. They validate the capability first on the small Qwen3.5-4B model, where the default routing threshold of zero point five gives an average performance loss of only zero point nine percent across the eight benchmarks, and then scale to Qwen3.8-27B using over thirty thousand A100 GPU-hours, where the average loss is one point zero eight percent. The point-adaptivity is the part that distinguishes it from a fixed-ratio pruner. On the twenty-seven-billion model the savings vary by benchmark, from sixteen point five percent on InfoVQA to fifty five point one percent on ScreenSpotV2, and on ScreenSpotV2 it loses only three point one seven percent despite that aggressive saving, while the fixed-ratio VisionZip baseline loses forty seven point eight eight percent on the same benchmark at a far lower saving. The paper includes a visual analysis of the routing: on a GUI grounding example, the FastV-style pruner drops the tokens covering the actual click targets, including a star button, while VisionWeave keeps every region represented, which is the concrete failure mode the content-adaptive router is designed to avoid. The serving-integration result is also worth calling out on its own, because the token savings only matter if they survive the serving stack, and the paper's SGLang numbers are measured end-to-end rather than as a raw FLOPs count, so they capture the real overhead of the extra vision-transformer reads and the cross-attention fusion loop, which is exactly the cost a deployment team would actually pay.
The skeptical read. First, the whole result is trained on and evaluated against the Qwen family, so the claim that this is a native capability of frontier MLLMs is really a claim about the Qwen line, and whether the self-distilled router transfers to a differently-architectured VLM is untested. Second, self-distillation means the ceiling is the native model's own output distribution, so the method can match the native model's quality but cannot exceed it, it is purely an efficiency technique, and the ninety eight point nine percent retention is measured against a baseline the authors also control. Third, the savings percentages are averages, and the paper's own point-adaptivity table shows the per-benchmark loss varies, with document and GUI grounding tasks, where fine detail is concentrated, being the ones where a misroute is most expensive. Bottom line: VisionWeave is a clean, well-motivated efficiency contribution, and the combination of content-adaptive savings, inference-time control without retraining, and a two point three times serving speedup with sub-one-point accuracy loss is exactly the kind of result an inference team can act on. I would want to see it on a non-Qwen VLM and on adversarial detail-dense inputs before trusting the near-native retention at the aggressive end of the threshold range.
