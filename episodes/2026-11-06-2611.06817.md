Open-HumanWAM, An Open Recipe for Scaling Human and Robot Experience for Embodied Foundation Models, is from Juncheng Ma, Jiaxin Li, and Jiankai Tu, with a large multi-institution team spanning Peking University, the National University of Singapore, MIT, UCSB, and NVIDIA. This is the open-recipe paper of the day, and the reason it is covered is that it is the first fully open recipe for scaling egocentric human pretraining to fifty thousand hours, which is exactly the open-training-recipe category the show tracks.

The premise is that egocentric human video is a scalable source of manipulation priors for robot learning, but translating raw data scale into generalizable robot policies has always been the hard part, because it demands rigorous data curation and a real training recipe, not just more video. The contribution is Open-HumanWAM-50K, a curated egocentric video corpus densely annotated with hand motion and natural language, and the open recipe for using it. The corpus, the curation and pretraining pipelines, the pretrained and post-trained checkpoints, the inference code, and the simulation evaluation suites are all open-sourced, which is the part that makes this a recipe rather than a result.

The model family is a world-action model, which jointly generates future video and actions from a single observation and a language instruction. The backbone extends Wan2.2-TI2V-5B, a video diffusion model, with an action expert that has the same depth as the video backbone, forming a Mixture-of-Transformers architecture. They apply an isolated attention mask in most experiments, allowing action tokens to attend to the first video frame but not to future video frames. The practical consequence of that mask is that at inference time the model can omit future video generation entirely and denoise only the action chunk, which is what makes the policy fast to run. The action space is a unified sixteen-dimensional bimanual end-effector representation, with delta poses computed in the camera coordinate frame relative to the first-frame observation.

The scaling study is the intellectual core, and it is the part worth understanding before you trust the downstream numbers. They pretrain on egocentric corpora of different sizes with the same recipe, then adapt each to RoboTwin 2.0 using clean demonstrations and measure task success in closed-loop evaluation, with standardized tasks, controlled environmental variation, and shared evaluation seeds. The finding is an empirical log-linear scaling law linking pretraining data hours to downstream robot success rates. That is the key result, and it is the kind of result that is genuinely useful for planning, because it tells you the return on additional egocentric data hours in a form you can extrapolate. They also study scaling diversity, not just hours, which is the more nuanced finding, because more hours of a narrow distribution of human video does not scale the same way as the same hours spread across more diverse scenes and tasks.

The concrete results split into the pure-human-pretraining model and the hybrid model. HumanWAM-Vanilla, pretrained exclusively on fifty thousand hours of egocentric human video, reaches ninety-two point zero eight percent success on RoboTwin 2.0 Clean2Clean and sixty-eight point eight eight percent on Clean2Random, alongside a fourteen point one seven percent mean success rate and a nineteen point zero zero mean progress score on RoboDojo. Then HumanWAM-Goal, a visual-goal-conditioned world-action model that does joint human-robot pretraining, pushes the simulation results to a new state of the art, ninety-two point seven zero percent on Clean2Clean and a substantial leap to eighty-four point six zero percent on Clean2Random. That jump on the randomized setting, from about sixty-nine to about eighty-five, is the important number, because the randomized setting is where generalization is actually tested, and it is where pure-human pretraining leaves the most on the table. On physical robots, HumanWAM-Goal exhibits emergent embodied in-context learning, adapting its manipulation behavior to unseen visual goals without parameter updates.

The training setup is specified at the level you would need to reproduce it. AdamW optimizer, peak learning rate of ten to the minus four, weight decay of zero point zero one, a cosine schedule over one hundred thousand steps with a linear warm-up over the first five percent of steps. The training budget is adapted to the corpus size, and they note that smaller corpora saturate earlier, which is consistent with the scaling law. The hybrid action representation and the HumanWAM-Goal architecture are the additions that unlock the broader generalization, and the training recipe for the hybrid model is specified separately.

The open-sourcing is the part that changes what this paper is for the field, and it is worth being explicit about the scope of what is released. The corpus is open, which is the scarce asset, because fifty thousand hours of densely annotated egocentric video with hand motion and natural language is not something a lab can casually reproduce. The curation and pretraining pipelines are open, so the data-cleaning and annotation steps are inspectable rather than a black box. The pretrained and post-trained checkpoints are on Hugging Face, the inference code is public, and the simulation evaluation suites are provided, which means the closed-loop evaluation protocol is reproducible rather than something you have to reverse-engineer from a results table. That combination, corpus plus recipe plus checkpoints plus eval harness, is what separates a recipe paper from a result paper, and it is the reason this is the one to watch if you are planning an embodied pretraining effort of your own, because the failure modes you would otherwise discover by burning compute are already documented here.

A skeptical read has a few things I would verify. The simulation results, even the state-of-the-art ones, are on RoboTwin 2.0 and RoboDojo, which are simulation benchmarks, and the gap between closed-loop simulation success and physical-robot success is the thing that has burned a lot of embodied-AI teams, so I would weight the physical-robot in-context-learning result, the one about adapting to unseen visual goals without updates, more than the simulation percentages, even though it is the less quantified of the two. The log-linear scaling law is the most reusable artifact, but it is fit on a specific backbone, a specific action representation, and a specific post-training protocol, and the paper's own diversity finding warns that the law is in hours-times-diversity, not hours alone, so extrapolating it to a different data source or a different robot embodiment is not a free step. The hybrid model's big gain on Clean2Random is the number that would change my prior, and I would want to see the ablation that isolates how much of that gain is the hybrid pretraining versus the goal-conditioning versus the post-training data, because those three are confounded in the headline comparison.

The engineering gotcha for reproducing this is the unified action representation and the cross-source action alignment. The sixteen-dimensional bimanual end-effector space is a specific choice, and the paper has a whole section on whether cross-source action alignment matters, which signals that aligning the human hand-motion annotations to the robot action space is a non-trivial design decision, not a detail. If you are building your own egocentric-to-robot pipeline, the action alignment step is where the human priors either transfer or silently fail to, and it is the first thing to get right, before you spend the compute on the fifty-thousand-hour pretrain.

Bottom line: Open-HumanWAM is a genuinely open embodied-foundation recipe, the first to scale egocentric human pretraining to fifty thousand hours with a published log-linear scaling law, open checkpoints, and a hybrid human-robot model that reaches a new simulation state of the art with a large gain on the randomized generalization setting. The main caveats are that the headline numbers are simulation-centric, the scaling law is specific to one backbone and action representation, and the hybrid gain is a confounded comparison, so before you bank on the transfer to your own robot, isolate the physical-robot in-context-learning result and the action-alignment ablation.
