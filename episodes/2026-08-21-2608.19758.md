FlashPrefill V2: Block-Sparse Prefill Attention for Long-Context LLM Serving, by Qihang Fan, Huaibo Huang, and Zhiying Wu, with Bingning Wang as project leader and Ran He. The affiliations are the Institute of Automation of the Chinese Academy of Sciences through its MAIS and NLPR labs, the University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, and WeChat at Tencent; the first author completed the work during an internship at WeChat.

Context for the audience: attention cost during prefill grows with the square of sequence length, and every training-free sparse-attention method lives or dies on three practical questions. Does accuracy survive aggressive sparsity? Is the kernel competitive with current dense implementations rather than last generation's? And does it work inside a real serving stack with paged KV cache and continuous batching? Their previous FlashPrefill answered none of those fully. It discovered patterns instantaneously using mean-pooled keys and max-based dynamic thresholding, avoiding the global sorting that Top-k selection requires, but accuracy degraded without bound under extreme sparsity, the kernel was built on FlashAttention-2's execution model, which leaves Hopper tensor-core throughput on the table, and it assumed a contiguous KV layout incompatible with any modern serving framework. V2 addresses exactly these three gaps.

First, the mean correction term. The original method truncates both sums of the softmax to selected blocks, so the discarded probability mass becomes output error once density drops low enough. V2 instead compensates each pruned block with its pooled statistics: precompute the block-mean key, block-mean value, and the query's score against the mean key scaled by the square root of head dimension, then add one surrogate contribution per pruned block into numerator and denominator, weighted by the block's token count. In implementation terms, each corrected block enters the kernel as one extra pipeline iteration whose logit is shifted by the natural logarithm of the block size, reusing the existing matrix-multiply and online-softmax machinery with no additional kernel launch; only blocks fully visible to the whole query tile get corrected, diagonal-band blocks being covered by forced selections anyway. An error analysis bounds the shift: max-based thresholding caps each pruned block's mass share, the residual error is a first-order within-block covariance between logit deviations and value deviations, vanishing when those are independent, and relative to dropping blocks outright the correction cancels the zeroth-order term entirely. Empirically this matters most in FP8: without correction the quantized variant loses 6.2 points at 128K on RULER; with it, everything stays within about 1.5 points of full attention.

Second, the kernel rewrite on CUTLASS and CuTe. Four pieces. PackGQA reshapes the query matrix from position-by-head layout to packed rows per key-value-head group, so all query heads in a group sharing one staged KV block consume it from shared memory rather than relying on cross-thread-block cache retention, shrinking the sparse index metadata by the group ratio and keeping tiles full even for short queries. Warp specialization partitions warp groups into one producer issuing asynchronous TMA or cp.async loads into a multi-stage shared-memory pipeline and two consumers running warpgroup-level matrix multiplies, with registers statically reallocated between roles: 24 registers for the producer with TMA, 240 for consumers, on a shared tile configuration of 128 by 64. Within each consumer, GEMM and softmax interleave pingpong fashion, issue the QK product of one block while accumulating the PV product of the previous and rescaling the softmax of the one before, overlapping tensor-core and exponentiation units. Index-driven traversal walks a CSR list of selected blocks backwards so producer and consumer evaluate identical sequences by construction and never synchronize for skipping, with next indices prefetched behind in-flight loads; under KV splitting, the CSR list is partitioned by selected-block count rather than position range, equalizing real work across splits where the dense heuristic would serialize heavy tail tiles. Scoring itself fuses into a single pass writing block energies bitcast into the index buffer, eliminating both a separate score buffer and a second GEMM pass. FP8 support uses e4m3 operands with per-tensor scales dequantized inside the softmax and probabilities mapped onto the full 0-to-256 range via a constant offset that cancels in the softmax ratio; because FP8 matrix multiplies mandate a transposed layout for the second operand, column-major values are consumed directly with the probability fragment rearranged purely in registers through warp shuffles and byte permutation, while row-major values transpose inside shared memory with deliberate bank-conflict avoidance.

Third, serving integration. KV addressing goes through a page table resolved on the fly with cp.async at arbitrary page size, and a varlen-aware persistent scheduler enumerates work units from per-request lengths, which is precisely the continuous-batching execution model. Integrated into SGLang as a standard attention backend, prefill runs sparse while decode falls back to dense since single-query decoding has no room for block sparsity; selection parameters are server arguments, per-step metadata builds once and shares across layers, and no model definition, KV layout, or scheduling change is required.

Numbers, measured on NVIDIA H20 GPUs at batch size 4, speedups end-to-end including pattern discovery and correction. Against FlashAttention-2 at 128K: 27.19 times in BF16 and 47.26 times in FP8 on Qwen3-30B-A3B, versus FlashPrefill V1's 18.67, MInference at 2.76, and XAttention at 3.42. Against the much harder FA3/4-aligned dense baseline they replicate themselves: still 17.54 times BF16 and 30.49 times FP8 at 128K, accelerating from 4K upward. Accuracy holds: RULER averages of 91.76 versus 92.05 full attention on the 30B model, and 87.79 against 88.82 on Llama-3.1-8B, beating MInference, FlexPrefill, XAttention, and V1 across the board; LongBench shows the same ordering, with V2 slightly above full attention on some models. Retained density falls from roughly 70 percent at 4K to about 5 percent at 128K. Serving results: time-to-first-token reductions up to 3.4 times BF16 and 4.8 times FP8 at 128K under continuous batching, nearly batch-invariant, with chunked-prefill open-loop runs showing 2.96 times TTFT reduction at 16K chunks in FP8. Ablations confirm each piece earns its complexity: removing correction costs 0.9 points BF16 and 2.3 points FP8 on average, threshold sweeps show corrected pipelines flat within 2.7 points of full attention down to 5.2 percent density, and auxiliary FLOPs stay bounded near 19 percent even at 4 percent retention.

A worked example of the correction, since this is the part most likely to be reimplemented: for a pruned block of 128 tokens with mean key k-bar and mean value v-bar, the query contributes one exponential term of its score against k-bar, multiplied by 128, into both the numerator weighted by v-bar and the denominator, while selected blocks still contribute exact per-token terms. The two streams merge inside the running online-softmax rescale, so the output is the exact path plus a zero-order estimate of everything discarded. A comparison against NVIDIA's HPC-Ops block-sparse kernel shows why this design matters in production: that kernel stores a dense padded mask per query head costing 8 megabytes at 64K context regardless of density, versus 3 megabytes here at 9 percent density organized per KV head, has no correction path, serial GEMM-softmax overlap, and FP8-only execution.

The threshold ablation quantifies the operating envelope. Sweeping the selection threshold alpha from 0.2 down to 0.0125 on RULER at 64K in FP8 raises density from 5.2 to 23.6 percent; with correction enabled, scores stay within 2.7 points of full attention across the entire sweep, and at the default alpha of 0.1 within 2.3 points, whereas removing correction consistently costs another 2.7 to 5.4 points as density falls. The practical consequence is that compensation enlarges the usable sparsity range: alpha can be doubled to 0.2 at 64K, halving density relative to default, while retaining above 80 points.

Skeptical notes. All hardware numbers come from one GPU family, H20, whose relatively weak compute makes memory-bound sparsity look its best; A100 or B200 ratios will differ. Decode is untouched, so this accelerates prefill only. Accuracy claims rest on RULER and LongBench, synthetic retrieval-heavy suites where sparse attention is known to behave well; your agentic workloads may weight long-range dependencies differently. Bottom line: this is the first training-free sparse prefill operator I have seen close the kernel-generation gap while integrating cleanly with paged serving, and the mean-correction trick is cheap enough to steal independently. Validate on your own accelerator and traffic mix before committing production latency budgets.
