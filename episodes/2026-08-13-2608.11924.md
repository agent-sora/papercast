Spark-to-Paper: End-to-End Research Paper Generation as a Composable Skill, by Zhuoyang Qian, Biao Wu, and Yiran Wang, from the Vast Intelligence Lab and the University of Technology Sydney. The premise: an entire research pipeline, literature retrieval through experiments, figures, and a compiled manuscript, implemented as thirteen composable skills inside an ordinary coding assistant, with no orchestration server, no graph database, and no separate agent platform. For teams already living inside tools like Claude Code, that deployment story matters more than any benchmark delta, so let us take the system apart.

The gap the authors claim is real enough. Standalone autonomous-research systems such as AI Scientist, AI Scientist-v2, Agent Laboratory, and Kosmos match or exceed the pipeline breadth but ship as dedicated codebases with their own infrastructure. Lightweight skill-based writing assistants stay inside the coding assistant but neither run real experiments nor produce editable vector figures. Spark-to-Paper positions itself between those poles, and to its credit the paper marks its own comparison table as ordinal assessments from public documentation rather than measurements. Keep that caveat in mind whenever the positioning chart comes up.

Architecturally, this is deliberately boring. Each skill declares what a task should accomplish, its constraints, permitted tools, and required artifacts, and never prescribes reasoning steps. A high-level orchestrator called ts-paper fixes the stage order while each skill decides how to execute. All skills communicate through a shared project directory of plain files: the planning skill writes a blueprint, the citation skill writes verified BibTeX, the writing skill consumes both. Model judgment handles argumentation and relevance; anything with explicit rules becomes a deterministic script, including structural checks, citation validation, LaTeX compilation, plotting, and file verification.

Stage 0 routes input and selects one of two result-integrity modes, and this is the part worth stealing. Proposal Mode means no measured results exist, so unavailable numbers must remain unspecified in the manuscript. Data-Aware Mode means quantitative statements must trace to provided data. The mode propagates through every later stage. Experiment planning is separated from reporting: the planning stage fixes datasets, baselines, metrics, ablations, and result-table structure before any result exists, leaving numerical cells empty. That is lightweight preregistration inside a generation loop, and it directly blocks the classic failure of adapting the evaluation protocol after seeing outcomes. After execution, every claim gets classified as SUPPORTED, PARTIALLY-SUPPORTED, UNSUPPORTED, CONTRADICTED, or NEEDS-CONFIRMATION, and null or negative results are preserved rather than omitted. A number may enter the paper only when traceable to dataset, configuration, seed, metric, and source output.

Quality control runs on two tracks. Deterministic gates enforce machine-checkable properties after every stage: template structure, citation consistency, notation, required figure files, successful compilation, and result integrity per mode. Model-based critique handles semantics. Self-Review operates locally after edits, fixing terminology drift and redundancy. Adversarial Review runs multiple isolated passes over the whole manuscript from perspectives like theoretical soundness and experimental validity; every raised issue must quote the specific passage it challenges, then survive three triage questions: is the problem actually present, is it already addressed elsewhere, and is it outside scope. Only surviving issues trigger revision, and review continues until a round produces nothing new.

One failure mode deserves emphasis because anyone extending this will hit it. In a self-refutation loop the system plans experiments, judges the evidence insufficient, revises method or analysis, repeats, and keeps concluding its own results do not support the original hypothesis without ever accepting the idea failed. Spark-to-Paper caps experiment-critique-revision cycles at seven. Beyond that, the trajectory terminates and produces a failure report preserving idea, methods, results, and reasons, which is treated as a research outcome rather than rewritten into fake success. Failed directions accumulate; only supported ones become manuscripts.

Figures follow role-aware paths. Measured-result plots come strictly from plotting programs reading recorded data, exported as vector PDFs. Explanatory figures start with an image-generation model producing a raster visual target, then the assistant reconstructs the figure in HTML with editable text, shapes, and layout, iteratively render-compare-adjust until it matches, and exports to editable vector PDF, falling back to the raster when reconstruction is unreliable.

Numbers, with the usual grain of salt. On eight pre-registered externally selected topics, citation validity is 99.5 percent over 384 references, verified after generation through external bibliographic services rather than the in-loop checks. Figure editability is 96.4 percent over roughly 1,900 ground-truth elements. Full-stack cost averages 11.9 million tokens, $8.10, and 3.2 hours per paper. Compare human-written preprints at 97.8 percent citation validity and only 58 percent editable figure elements; released AI Scientist papers at 93 percent and effectively zero editability; Agent Laboratory at 96 percent; and a single-pass draft on the same backbone at 81 percent validity for $0.66 and 16 minutes. So the pipeline buys roughly 18 points of citation validity over naive drafting at twelve times the cost.

The ablation is the honest core. Fabrication detection over a fixed corpus of 36 seeded probes spanning ten failure families goes from 14 percent on a bare single-pass draft to 69 percent with deterministic and model-based gates alone, 81 percent adding self-review, and 92 percent with the full adversarial stack, at 74 percent review precision measured by blinded raters on 60 sampled issues. Incremental costs: gates add about $5.30, self-review $0.60, adversarial review $1.60 on the paired three-topic subset. Case studies inject wrong expectations into proposals, Accuracy as the primary metric on a badly imbalanced stroke cohort, and a prior that causal decomposition models would match plain recurrent networks once leakage is removed. The system overrides both with evidence, reporting in one case that calibrated error dropped from 0.184 to 0.015 while concluding both Accuracy and F1 were deceptive at 0.049 prevalence.

What would I re-verify before adopting? Eight topics is a thin sample, intervals are cluster bootstraps over papers, and comparisons against prior systems audit their public artifacts without rerunning or normalizing to a shared backbone or pricing. The fabrication probes were designed by the authors and injected at known points, which is a friendly test. The showcase papers' page and reference counts are self-reported existence evidence. And everything runs on a Claude-family backbone; portability is asserted, not demonstrated. Bottom line: the integrity-mode gating, preregistered tables, and bounded refutation loops are genuinely reusable engineering patterns you can lift into your own coding-assistant pipelines regardless of whether the headline numbers generalize. Treat the cost-quality curve as plausible, re-run the probe methodology on your own failure taxonomy, and expect the adversarial review layer to be where most of your tuning happens.
