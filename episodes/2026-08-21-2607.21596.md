FlowEvo: Self-Evolving Agents through the Co-Evolution of Workflows and Executable Skills, by Zeyu Ren, Ling Yue, and Ran Li, with Yishu Wang, Shengxiang Xu, Hanmo Liu, Shaowu Pan, and Shimin Di. The affiliations are Southeast University, Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute, and the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology.

The gap this paper identifies is real and worth articulating precisely. Workflow-based agents construct procedures at inference time: decomposition, tool calls, code generation, checking, repair. But whatever structure a successful episode discovers gets discarded when the episode ends. Skill libraries preserve reusable routines as executable objects, yet they are typically assembled offline for a fixed task distribution rather than growing from the agent's own problem solving. Text memories such as Agent Workflow Memory sit in between: they describe what worked but cannot be invoked or validated, so reuse still depends on the model re-deriving the procedure from prose. FlowEvo is a training-free framework that closes this loop: successful workflows are compiled into callable skill records stored in a persistent bank, and those skills feed back into later workflow construction either through direct execution or as structured context. Model weights never change.

The system decomposes into four components. A workflow planner generates plans given task and context. An executor with verification produces traces and outcomes. A trace-to-skill compiler extracts artifacts only from verifier-passed successful episodes: it identifies an entry point, infers a callable signature, extracts replay tests where possible, and tags provenance, task patterns, reuse constraints, and preferred usage mode. A skill registry maintains retrieval indices and lifecycle state. A skill record is a five-tuple of executable body, explicit interface, replay tests, metadata, and lifecycle state; admission runs interface compliance, functional correctness against replay tests and held-out checks, and safety compliance covering banned imports and banned calls. Records failing checks can be retained context-only or shadowed for diagnostics rather than deleted outright.

Routing has three branches, and the distinction between the last two is central to their mechanism analysis. Dynamic generation proceeds with no retrieved skill. Direct execution replays a validated callable on compatible tasks, permitted only after filtering and validation pass. Skill-conditioned generation injects a compact structured view, signature, task pattern, scaffold hint, short excerpt, into planning without replaying the artifact, used when retrieval is informative but not reliable enough for execution; injecting the compact view rather than full source bounds prompt overhead and makes gains attributable to structured reuse rather than unrestricted replay. Retrieval scores candidates on lexical overlap, task-pattern compatibility, interface compatibility, and historical utility, penalizing skills flagged by negative evidence.

Lifecycle control is the part most papers skip. For each actively used skill, FlowEvo periodically compares success rates of episodes where the skill was applied against matched episodes where it was withheld, a contrastive delta serving as a lightweight causal signal for negative transfer without holdout experiments. Persistently negative deltas trigger suppression, with conservative policies that re-check suspicious skills through targeted audit episodes and downgrade direct-use privileges before removal. One documented case: a pick-two-object-and-place skill on ALFWorld showed guided success of two out of fourteen against one out of three unguided, a delta of minus 0.19 below the minus 0.1 threshold, suppressed within fourteen guided episodes.

Evaluation uses full standard splits of five benchmarks under a shared GPT-4o-mini backbone: ALFWorld's out-of-distribution eval split with 134 tasks across six household task types and up to fifty steps per episode with binary terminal reward only; HumanEval at 164 problems; MBPP at 500; GSM8K at 1,319; MATH-500. Baselines span experience learning, ExpeL and self-generated in-context examples, and workflow optimization, ADAS, AFlow, MermaidFlow, EvoAgentX, DyFlow, and ORCH, all run from official code at default configuration with matched optimization budgets. Every benchmark starts from an empty bank with no cross-benchmark transfer, which makes the comparison coverage-oriented rather than transfer-oriented.

Headline numbers: highest accuracy on all five benchmarks among eight baselines. ALFWorld reaches 85.6 percent, plus or minus 3.8 over three seeds, 26.4 points above AFlow, while averaging 9,329 tokens per episode against roughly 30 thousand for every baseline. HumanEval hits 95.1 percent at 880 tokens versus ExpeL's 89.0. GSM8K reaches 97.1 at 541 tokens. MATH-500 posts 75.9, above EvoAgentX at 73.6. MBPP lands at 79.6, though here token cost is merely comparable to ADAS rather than lower, the one setting without Pareto dominance over the workflow baselines. The scale ladder across GPT-4.1-nano, mini, and full shows gains widening as models shrink, reaching plus 53.2 points on ALFWorld for the smallest model, and across a breadth panel spanning seven more families from Qwen2.5-7B to DeepSeek-V3.1, FlowEvo beats ExpeL in 49 of 50 model-dataset cells with mean gain plus 11.0 and median plus 7.0. The single loss, Qwen3-8B on MATH-500 at minus 10.0, is diagnostic: interface-violating skill bodies got rejected at admission, leaving the bank too sparse to support retrieval, marking the bootstrap floor.

Ablations attribute the gain cleanly on ALFWorld starting from ReAct at 33.6 percent: compilation alone adds 5.2 points even without reuse, because extracting regular structure improves auxiliary memory quality; enabling skill feedback jumps to 80.6, plus 41.8, the dominant term; curation adds the final 5.0. Robustness checks matter for anyone deploying this: removing the verifier-pass gate costs only 1.5 points, flipping 20 percent of pass-fail labels both directions costs 1.7, neither significant under McNemar's test, because compile-time verification pairs with contrastive checking during use. Bank dynamics stay bounded: across 134 episodes the ALFWorld bank saturates at seven workflow templates and eighteen exemplars, first-reuse occurs around episode ten, cumulative direct reuse reaches 101 of 134 episodes with a hit rate near 75 percent, and 99 of those 101 direct reuses succeed. Per-task-type analysis shows gains tracking direct-hit rates, from plus 78 points where replay completes 83 percent of tasks down to plus 28 where the skill matches but never executes directly and conditioning does the work. Fixed-bank MBPP analysis isolates modes: on exact-transfer cases executable reuse beats dynamic generation on accuracy and cost, 85.9 percent at 204 tokens versus 81.7 at 224, while text workflow memory trades 1.4 points for 85 percent more tokens.

The extended baseline panel fills in the picture on code and math. Against MermaidFlow and ORCH, both at 87.8 on HumanEval, FlowEvo's 95.1 is a 7.3-point margin at less than a fifth of AFlow's token cost. On GSM8K, ORCH is closest at 93.9 followed by EvoAgentX at 93.0. On MATH-500 FlowEvo leads ADAS and AFlow on all seven subjects, with per-subject tables in the appendix. The authors are careful about one thing: baselines ran from official released code at default configuration under matched search budgets, so some recent systems underperform their published numbers for reasons of configuration rather than capability.

Two implementation details worth carrying over. First, the formal loop: at episode t the agent holds context consisting of the skill bank, auxiliary memory layers, and accumulated retrieval-routing-failure state; it retrieves, routes, optionally constructs a workflow plan, executes and verifies to obtain trace and outcome, then updates all three context components only when a successful episode yields reusable structure, so self-evolution happens entirely in memory while the base model stays frozen. Second, on code and math benchmarks FlowEvo combines skill compilation with adaptive escalation, engaging more expensive strategies only when earlier stages fail verification, which is where most of its token advantage comes from relative to fixed multi-stage pipelines that always run generation, testing, ensembling, review, and revision.

Skeptical read. Every benchmark here has clean verifiers, environment feedback or hidden tests; free-form settings remain out of scope, as the authors concede. Baselines ran at default configurations tuned for stronger backbones, so some workflow-baseline gaps may compress under tuning. And ALFWorld's recurring parametric structure is close to a best case for direct replay; the interesting number is the conditioned-generation contribution on unique problems, which is real but smaller. Bottom line: compiling verified traces into governed executable artifacts beats storing prose about them, and the contrastive-utility curation is what keeps accumulation honest. Expect the largest returns exactly where your base model is weakest.
