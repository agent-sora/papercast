RynnUnify: Towards Unified Embodied Brain, by Minghao Zhu, Bohan Hou, and Kun Xiang, from DAMO Academy at Alibaba Group and Hupan Lab. It is a 16B unified embodied model.

This is a foundation-model release, which is why it is on the show regardless of its upvote count. The claim is that a single 16B parameter model can serve as a shared base for three jobs that are usually separate products: embodied understanding, reading the physical state of a scene and grounding objects in it; long-horizon visual planning, predicting how the scene will evolve across a sequence of subtasks; and robot control, turning those predictions into actions. The unifying idea is interleaved video-language reasoning. The model does not just answer questions about a video; it generates future video frames as part of its reasoning, so the planning capability is grounded in predicted pixels rather than in a latent plan nobody can inspect. That is the architectural bet of the paper: video prediction is a first-class reasoning primitive, not a side task.

The 16B is not a single dense transformer. RynnUnify is built on the Mixture-of-Transformers architecture from Cosmos 3, split into an 8B autoregressive reasoner and an 8B diffusion generator that predicts continuous visual latents through rectified flow. The two branches have separate parameters and interact through joint attention, so the generator can attend to the reasoner's representations while producing video. The reasoner is retained from Cosmos 3 unchanged, and the paper's architectural modifications are on the generator side: its visual conditioning is extended with cross-chunk scene memory and first-and-last-frame-conditioned generation, which is what gives the video rollout persistent scene references and continuity across successive subtasks. That split matters for cost: the autoregressive branch carries the reasoning and the tool-call-like decisions, while the diffusion branch pays the pixel cost only when a prediction is actually being made.

The data recipe is also worth noting. For general understanding, the fine-tuning uses a 10 percent subset of the RynnBrain pretraining corpus, covering image and video understanding, visual question answering, temporal reasoning, and instruction following, plus embodied cognition tasks, object understanding, spatial reasoning, counting, and egocentric task understanding, with spatially grounded supervision for object, area, and affordance localization, trajectory prediction, and manipulation planning. Reusing an existing corpus at 10 percent for a 16B model is a deliberate choice to stay in a regime where the understanding capabilities come from the inherited pretraining rather than from the new fine-tune, and it is the reason the understanding benchmark results are what they are.

The model has two coupled branches. The reasoner is a language-side transformer that reads text, visual tokens, and scene memory, and emits reasoning and subtask structure. The generator branch predicts video. Visual inputs are encoded by a causal video VAE with spatial compression 16 and temporal compression 4, then patchified into visual tokens. A detail that matters for long horizons: scene memory. Up to four reference frames are encoded independently into token groups and placed after the reasoner's textual context and before the main video tokens. So the model carries an explicit visual short-term memory of the scene as it reasons forward, which is how it stays grounded over many subtasks instead of drifting. The pretraining is extensive: general multimodal understanding, spatial reasoning, physical prediction, and affordance grounding, plus subtask planning, world-modeling descriptions, and question answering with intermediate video context. The point of the last item is that the model is trained to emit and then interpret its own intermediate video predictions, which is what makes the video reasoning loop usable at inference time.

On top of the base model, two extensions are trained. The agentic extension post-trains an additional 8B agent from the reasoner with reinforcement learning and final-answer feedback, on top of the 10-percent-corpus understanding fine-tune described above, so the RL stage is a small post-training stage rather than a from-scratch training run. This agent learns when to answer directly and when to call the frozen RynnUnify model to generate a video and then interpret it. The decision to invoke video generation is learned, which is the important part. The model is not always paying the cost of video rollout; it has learned to reserve it for questions where the unobserved future state actually matters, such as what will happen to an object once support is removed. The world-action extension, or WAM, takes the full model and adds latent planning plus joint video-action training, so the same weights that predict the scene also emit robot actions. The paper introduces two new evaluation suites for these capabilities, Rynn-VP for long-horizon visual planning and Rynn-GEU for anticipatory reasoning, which is notable because it means the claims are measured on benchmarks built for exactly this capability, not on borrowed general video QA.

The numbers. On embodied understanding, across the six spatial and grounding benchmarks they report, VSI-Bench, MMSI, ERQA, RoboSpatial, EmbSpatial, and RefSpatial, RynnUnify scores 67.1, 35.9, 43.8, 72.8, 83.0, and 60.3, an average of 57.9, which the paper states is the highest average among the evaluated unified multimodal models, ahead of BAGEL, RxBrain, and Cosmos 3 Nano. On Rynn-VP, the long-horizon visual planning benchmark, RynnUnify improves the overall score by 9.75 points over Cosmos 3 Nano. The agentic extension improves the Rynn-GEU average by 6.0 points over the direct-reasoning baseline, which is the measured value of the learned when-to-predict policy. On the action side, the WAM extension achieves 51.5 percent success on 20 selected composite RoboCasa365 simulation tasks, and 46.7 percent average success across three real-robot tasks on the Astribot S1 arm, including multi-step manipulation with body reorientation and history-dependent execution. For scale context, the real-robot comparison sets include pi-0.5 and GR00T, where RynnUnify reports 46.7 percent average success against 25.0 for pi-0.5 and 26.7 for GR00T on that suite.

A few engineering notes for anyone planning to build on this. The joint-attention coupling means the reasoner's context grows with every predicted video chunk, so long-horizon planning is bounded by context length as much as by the model's world knowledge, and the scene-memory token groups are a deliberate mitigation of that growth. The rectified-flow generator predicts continuous latents rather than discrete tokens, which is what keeps the video rollout cheap enough to invoke mid-reasoning, and the first-and-last-frame conditioning is what makes successive subtask rollouts visually continuous instead of restarting from scratch. The Astribot S1 real-robot tasks are the most externally verifiable numbers in the paper: three tasks, multi-step, with body reorientation, and the comparison against pi-0.5 and GR00T on the same rig is the number to cite if you want one. The 8B agentic agent that learned to invoke video selectively is also the cheapest thing to reproduce from this stack, since it is a post-trained reasoner variant with RL on final-answer feedback, and its 6.0 point Rynn-GEU gain is the measured value of the selectivity policy itself.

The skeptical read. This is a capability-stack release, and the honest way to read it is as three separate claims bound by one model. The understanding claim is the strongest: a 57.9 average that leads the comparison set on a unified-model basis. The planning claim is the most interesting and the least established, because Rynn-VP is the authors' own benchmark, and a 9.75 point margin over Cosmos 3 Nano on a self-built suite is suggestive but not yet a community-standard result. The action claim has the clearest external grounding, real-robot tasks with named baselines, and the 46.7 percent average is a real number, but 46.7 percent success on real manipulation is still far from deployment, and the 20 selected RoboCasa tasks are a curated set. The video-as-reasoning-primitive bet is the thing to watch. If interleaved video generation keeps paying for itself as a planning tool, this 16B model is an early data point that the unification is real; if the gains evaporate off the authors' own benchmarks, it is a large model with three fine-tuned heads. The 8B agentic agent that learns when to predict is the most reusable design pattern here, even for teams that never touch the base model. Trust the embodied-understanding numbers and the real-robot deltas. Treat the Rynn-VP and Rynn-GEU margins as promising but self-referential until independent replication lands.
