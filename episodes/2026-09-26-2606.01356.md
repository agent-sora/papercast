A Formally Verified Library of Mathematical Finance in Lean 4, by Raphael
Coelho, an independent researcher who does not list an institutional
affiliation on the paper, which is itself a notable detail about the kind of
work that is becoming possible with proof assistants, one person building a
machine-checked library of a substantial field of mathematics.

The setting is the broader trend of interactive theorem provers moving over
the last decade from isolated landmark proofs, a single celebrated result
discharged in a formal system, toward broad reusable libraries, where the
point is not one theorem but a body of mathematics that other formal
developments can build on. The paper positions itself against that backdrop:
Mathlib, the community library for Lean 4, now carries a substantial
measure-theoretic probability development, conditional expectation,
martingales, the Radon-Nikodym theorem, Doob's theorems, and recent work has
begun to build continuous-time stochastic processes on top of it, including a
Brownian motion package. What this paper does is take that continuous-time
foundation and extend it into the actual mathematics of finance, which is a
meaningful step because finance is one of the fields where the continuous-
time theory is used daily but where, before this work, there was no
comprehensive machine-checked development of it.

The scope is the headline. The library contains two hundred fifty-one
sorry-free theorems, and sorry-free is the phrase that matters, because in
Lean a sorry is an explicit hole in a proof, a place where the theorem is
stated but not actually proved, and a library that is sorry-free is one in
which every result is fully discharged by a proof the assistant has checked.
The two hundred fifty-one break down into two hundred four full
results, nineteen library wrappers, and twenty-eight reduced-core entries, all
over roughly twenty-four thousand eight hundred lines of Lean. The total of
two hundred fifty-one theorems is not a trivial body of work, and the breadth
spans
eleven areas, from the measure-theoretic foundations of continuous-time
stochastic calculus, through derivative pricing, to applied risk, portfolio
theory, and fixed-income theory. The paper is explicit that breadth is the
setting and not the point, and it names two things that make the library more
than a catalogue. The first is that it reaches into the continuous theory far
enough to construct the L2 Itô integral as a bounded linear isometry,
which is a specific and non-trivial construction, because the Itô integral is
the central object of continuous-time stochastic calculus and building it as
an isometry is the kind of result that anchors the rest of the theory. The
paper even records the worked capstone identity, that the Itô integral of
Brownian motion against itself recovers one half of the difference between the
terminal and initial squared Brownian values minus the elapsed time, which is
the integration-by-parts identity that the whole pricing theory leans on, and
having it machine-checked rather than hand-verified is the difference the
library is designed to make. The
second is that it derives the risk-neutral pricing measure rather than assuming
it, which is a methodological choice that matters in finance specifically,
because the risk-neutral measure is the object that connects the real-world
probability measure to the pricing measure, and deriving it means the
relationship is proved rather than taken as a starting axiom.

The third and most distinctive contribution is what the paper calls a
faithfulness audit, and this is the part I would flag for anyone skeptical of
formal libraries in general. The concern with any formal development is that
the Lean statement might not actually say what the informal mathematical
claim intends, that the formalization is technically correct but subtly
shifts the mathematics. This library addresses that directly: every result is
classified by how its Lean statement relates to the mathematics it claims to
express, and a build-enforced gate pins down the axioms that each proof
actually uses, so a reader can see precisely what has been proved unconditionally
and what has only been proved under added hypotheses. That build-enforced gate
is important because it means the axiom dependence is not a documentation
afterthought, it is something the build process checks, and it catches the
failure mode where a proof silently relies on an axiom that was never
intended to be in the base theory. The paper reports that the audited results
rest on only Lean's three standard axioms, propositional extensionality,
classical choice, and quotient soundness, which is a constrained and
inspectable set, and the point of the gate is that a future
contributor who adds a proof that quietly leans on a stronger principle will
have the build flag it rather than have it slip in unnoticed.

The paper closes with a candid and, I think, important finding, and it is worth
quoting the spirit of it directly. A formal base over classical financial
mathematics yields certified unification of known results rather than new
financial theory. The contribution, the author says, is methodological and
infrastructural, reusable verified foundations for mathematical finance
together with the faithfulness audit, not a new financial result. That is an
honest framing that a lot of formal-mathematics work would do well to adopt,
because the temptation in this area is to overclaim, to present a library as
if it had discovered new mathematics when what it has actually done is make
existing mathematics machine-checkable, and the difference matters for how the
work is valued and for how much trust it should be given.

Now the skeptical read. The first question is the single-author scope, and I
say that not as a criticism of the quality but as a question about
maintainability and coverage. A library of this size, built by one person, has
the coverage of one person's judgment about which results matter and which
formalizations are the most useful, and the eleven areas are a selection, not
the whole of mathematical finance. The second question is the depth versus
breadth trade-off, and the paper is honest about it, the continuous-time part
reaches to the Itô integral and the risk-neutral measure but that is a
specific depth, and a reader working on a part of the theory the library does
not cover cannot assume the foundations there are equally solid. The third
question is the faithfulness audit itself, and the honest version of that
question is whether the classification of each Lean statement against its
informal claim is machine-checked or is a human judgment recorded in the
library, because if it is a human judgment, it is a good and valuable one, but
it is not the same kind of guarantee as the sorry-free proofs, and the reader
should know which results have which kind of backing. And the practical
question is adoption, because the value of a formal library is realized only
when other people build on it, and a single-author library's future depends on
whether the community finds the formalizations natural enough to extend
rather than rewrite. And the reproducibility story is unusually concrete for
this kind of work, the paper pins the exact toolchain, a specific Lean release
candidate and a specific Mathlib commit, so that anyone who wants to check
the sorry-free claim can do it against a fixed, named state of the community
library rather than against whatever Mathlib happens to be at the time of
reading, which matters because Mathlib is actively developed and the set of
axioms and lemmas available shifts from commit to commit.

Bottom line: this is a substantial and unusually honest formal-mathematics
contribution, two hundred fifty-one sorry-free theorems across eleven
areas of mathematical finance, with a continuous-time core that constructs the
Itô integral as an isometry and derives the risk-neutral measure, and a
faithfulness audit with a build-enforced axiom gate that directly addresses
the formalization-faithfulness concern. The open question is whether the
single-author scope limits its coverage and maintainability, and whether the
community adopts it as a foundation or treats it as a demonstration of what
the approach can do.
