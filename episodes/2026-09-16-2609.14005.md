Paper four is the StepAudio 3 Realtime Technical Report, from the StepFun Audio Team, a 90-author report from StepFun, the Chinese AI lab behind the Step series of models. This is a systems-and-training report on a new audio-language foundation model built for realtime spoken interaction, and it is the day's foundation-model release, so it gets an episode on the standing rule regardless of its 21 upvotes. The headline claim is that the model can reason deeply while still speaking in real time, which is the tension every speech model has to solve: deliberation takes time, and conversation punishes silence.

The architecture is a mixture-of-experts language model with about 196 billion total parameters and 11 billion active per token, built on the Step 3.7 Flash backbone. The audio frontend is the Audio Transformer encoder from Qwen3-Omni, with an adapter that maps encoder outputs into the language model's representation space. The full-duplex input path carries both the user's audio stream and the model's own audio stream, so the model hears itself while it listens. A speech generator produces streaming model audio with context-appropriate tone, pauses, and hesitation, and that output feeds back into the model audio stream for subsequent turns.

The whole system is organized around what the authors call the listen-converse-think-act loop, with four named components. Deep Perception captures both the linguistic content and the nonverbal acoustic evidence, the how as well as the what. Seamless Duplex manages the conversational floor: it distinguishes a pause before a request is complete, an acknowledgment backchannel, and a substantive interruption, and handles yielding the floor. Think-While-Speaking is the central contribution, and it coordinates reasoning with delivery by letting spoken output begin before the full reasoning trace is complete. Adaptive Thinking decides when explicit reasoning is worth the latency, and multi-token prediction, specifically the Medusa-style approach, accelerates the private reasoning. A streaming voice agent carries conversational intent into asynchronous tool execution, so the user can keep talking while an external task runs, and the tool results come back into the conversation when they land.

The training recipe is three stages. Pretraining runs modality alignment, then multimodal mixed training, then a cooldown on higher-quality data, at a fixed 32K sequence length over 1.2 trillion training tokens, with the mixture weighted toward more pure text to preserve the base language model's general capabilities for the reasoning and agent training that comes later. Midtraining extends the context to 128K and leans on perception, synthetic conversational, and voice-agent data, increasing the share of audio understanding and agent interaction. The pretraining pipeline itself is an automated large-scale audio curation system: sound event detection and voice activity detection filter raw audio, samples are resegmented to preserve semantic completeness, multiple recognition systems transcribe and cross-check, and each sample gets quality grades used for quality-aware sampling.

The conversational-intelligence layer sits on top of the loop. Seamless Duplex decides when the model should respond, and conversational intelligence decides how it engages and how much reasoning the response needs. Routine turns are supposed to skip deliberation entirely, while complex requests retain the reasoning required for a reliable answer. That is the role of Adaptive Thinking, the controller that decides whether a given turn uses explicit reasoning at all, with multi-token prediction cutting the decoding cost of the turns where it does. The design point is that the system is not paying a fixed reasoning tax on every utterance, and that is the right architecture for a voice assistant where most turns are short.

The numbers. On audio understanding, the realtime model reports 90.6 on MMSU, 86.5 on MMAR, 59.7 on MultiChallenge, and 49.3 on AudioMultiChallenge, compared with baselines including Doubao 2.0 Lite, Gemini 3 Flash, and Gemini 3.1 Pro, with Gemini 3.1 Pro ahead on two of the four. On dialogue, it reports a 73.0 macro average on StepAudioChat in reasoning mode and 70.4 in the interactive realtime test, versus 77.1 for Kimi K3 in reasoning mode, so the dedicated reasoning models still lead on their own benchmarks. On full-duplex behavior it reports 98.9 overall on the Artificial Analysis Full-Duplex Bench, ahead of GPT-realtime-2 at 95.3 and Qwen Audio 3.0 Realtime Plus at 98.4. On agentic tool use, tau-Voice, it reports a 56.0 percent macro task-success rate, roughly level with the best reported baseline at 56.5 and ahead of GPT-Realtime-2.1 at 45.7. The companion ASR model, StepAudio 3 ASR Max, reports 1.18 percent word error rate on LibriSpeech clean, 0.49 on AISHELL-1, and 4.35 on WenetSpeech meeting. The paper is candid about remaining gaps in multi-turn constraint following and retail tool-use tasks.

The full-duplex floor management is worth more detail because it is the part that separates this from a half-duplex request-response system. In a half-duplex design the model either listens or speaks, and the turn boundary is explicit. Here the user stream and the model stream are always both live, and the system decides, from acoustic evidence, semantic context, and dialogue history, whether to keep listening, start responding, keep speaking, or yield the floor. It combines acoustic timing with semantic completeness to tell a within-turn pause apart from a turn ending. During model speech it interprets user backchannels in context: a brief acknowledgment signals continued engagement without a request for the floor, while a substantive correction or request signals an intent to interrupt. It also uses dialogue history to decide whether incoming speech is addressed to the assistant at all, which is how it rejects background conversation. The training for this is concrete: midtraining supervises streaming ASR, voice activity detection, and streaming prediction of utterance completeness, on top of over 10,000 hours of synthetic full-duplex interaction data, and post-training refines turn taking, backchannel handling, interruption handling, and background speech rejection. The AA full-duplex score breaks down as 100.0 on turn taking, 99.0 on user interruption handling, 98.9 on pause handling, and 98.0 on backchannel handling.

The voice agent deserves a sentence on how it stays out of the way. When the user makes a request that requires a tool, the agent resolves the request and its arguments, fires the execution, and then, crucially, does not block the dialogue. The user can keep speaking, ask a follow-up, or change course while the external task runs, and when the tool result lands it is inserted into the conversational context and referenced in the next turn. The failure mode this design has to avoid is the stale-result problem, where the user has already moved on by the time the result arrives, and the paper does not quantify how often tool calls outlast the user's patience.

My assessment: the interesting engineering claim is Think-While-Speaking, decoupling the pace of spoken delivery from the pace of private reasoning, and the full-duplex result of 98.9 is the number that matters for the product thesis. But three things to re-verify before trusting the comparison. The dialogue benchmark, StepAudioChat, is the vendor's own closed, text-based benchmark, and it measures text-level response quality while explicitly excluding prosody, turn timing, and interruption handling, so it says little about the realtime properties the model is sold on; the external numbers, Artificial Analysis and tau-Voice, are the ones worth citing, and on tau-Voice the lead is effectively a tie. The full-duplex bench is a third-party one, built from a subset of Full Duplex Bench v1 and v1.5, but it is a narrow slice of interaction quality, and near-saturated scores at 98 and 100 across categories leave little headroom to separate systems. And the report gives no latency figures anywhere in the pages I could verify, which is odd for a model whose entire pitch is realtime, so I would want first-token latency and interruption-recovery latency before I would call the loop truly realtime. The ASR numbers are strong and independently checkable against published error rates.

The bottom line: a substantial foundation-model release that pairs a credible full-duplex architecture with a think-while-speaking design, and whose strongest externally verifiable result is the full-duplex benchmark lead. The vendor-run dialogue benchmark and the missing latency numbers are the caveats I would close before adopting the system.
