Intern-S2-Mobius: Foundation Model with Decoupled Knowledge and Reasoning, by Kai Chen, Jifeng Ding, and Ning Ding, with the Intern-S2-Mobius Team at the Shanghai AI Laboratory. The claim is architectural and blunt: binding knowledge storage, the feed-forward network, to reasoning operators, self-attention, layer by layer inside every Transformer block wastes both compute and parameters. Mobius-v0 cuts that binding. One globally shared Memory, built from feed-forward parameters, stores knowledge vectors; multiple Reasoner layers, built from self-attention, iteratively query it through their hidden states. Every reasoning layer reads the entire knowledge repository rather than only what the previous layer passed down. The paper reports a seven-billion-parameter model trained from scratch matching a Transformer baseline's downstream score using sixty-two point six percent of the training data, and a thirty-five-billion production model converted from Qwen3.5 delivering nearly four times end-to-end inference speedup at comparable quality. Those are strong claims for an architecture paper, so the details deserve scrutiny.

Two design choices do the work. First, backward residual connection. Standard residuals are unidirectional: shallow states can be read by deep layers, but deep-layer attention processes only knowledge originating from shallow layers. If critical knowledge fails to activate early, the model compensates by generating low-information tokens in an explicit chain of thought and trying again next forward pass. A direct bidirectional residual is infrastructure-hostile, so Mobius gets the effect indirectly: since all Reasoners share one memory, every layer accesses everything, which functions as a backward path. Second, dynamic latent reasoning. Instead of decoding one token per full traversal of all layers, Mobius iterates hidden-state latents against the full knowledge store within just a few layers, refining a continuous vector that carries deliberation internally, then decodes multiple tokens synchronously at deeper layers via multi-token prediction heads. Deliberation, trial and error, and refinement move from emitted text into differentiable latent iterations. The authors position this as a synthesis of looped Transformers and diffusion language models, with higher loop frequency and no fixed decode schedule.

Implementation notes from the text: the shared memory is constructed by horizontal concatenation of FFN blocks, preserving key-value correspondence, because the FFN is treated as a key-value knowledge-vector pool. At larger scales this concatenation is partitioned MoE-style with sparse activation during forward passes, since naive full activation would make each pass prohibitively expensive. The cost profile is explicit: activation is highly sparse but memory-access pressure rises and per-pass forward efficiency drops, yet end-to-end throughput still lands near four times a same-parameter Transformer because outputs are markedly shorter.

Now the evidence, starting with training from scratch. Two 7B-A1B mixture-of-experts models, one Mobius and one Transformer, trained on one trillion tokens and compared on MMLU across checkpoints. Mobius tracks higher MMLU throughout, reaching the Transformer's final score at zero point six two six times the data, stated as one point six times data efficiency. The hypothesis offered is parameter redundancy: Transformers store the same knowledge redundantly across layers, while decoupled storage compresses better. That mechanism is asserted rather than demonstrated; treat it as such.

The production model matters more for practitioners. Intern-S2-Mobius-35B starts from Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, converts to Mobius architecture, continues pre-training on another one trillion tokens, then runs supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning. Benchmark table against Qwen3.5-35B: MMLU Pro eighty-nine point zero five versus eighty-five point three one, GPQA Diamond eighty point eight one versus eighty point two four, IMO Bench eighty-one point two five versus seventy-seven point five zero, AIME 2026 ninety-five point three one versus ninety-two point zero eight, HMMT 2026 eighty-five point five one versus seventy-eight point five zero, AMO fifty-eight versus fifty, SimpleQA twenty-eight point nine zero versus twenty-one point three nine; average sixty-seven point eight eight versus sixty-five point zero five. Losses exist: UGD hard drops to seventy-three point zero two from seventy-eight point zero two and HLE to nineteen point one one from twenty-two point four zero. Scientific benchmarks show the largest jumps: Biology-Instructions fifty-one point four zero against three point seven seven, MolecularIQ fifty-nine point two nine against twenty-nine point one three, molecular instructions forty-five point seven three against twenty-one point seven zero.

Appendix experiments add texture worth having before you dismiss or adopt this. The compositional generalization toy task, drawn from Physics of Language Models, trains on single-hop facts for five hundred entities plus two-hop facts for four hundred entities and tests two-hop questions on one hundred held-out entities, where an unreleased newer Mobius variant converges faster and scores higher than the Transformer baseline, direct evidence on cross-knowledge connection rather than memorization. Expert-activation heatmaps show the from-scratch model distributing experts fairly uniformly across reasoning layers while the converted thirty-five-billion model retains a pronounced block-diagonal routing pattern inherited from the source checkpoint, meaning architecture conversion alone does not fully reorganize how the shared memory gets used. Layerwise analysis under multi-token prediction shows Mobius forming more coherent semantic trajectories across intermediate layers, culminating in a fully accepted five-token draft where the baseline loses its draft after two tokens.

Efficiency results come with the right measurement. Request throughput aggregated across evaluation benchmarks shows roughly four point six times speedup at small batch sizes compressing toward two times at batch twenty-eight, with per-benchmark curves on MMLU Pro, GPQA Diamond, IMO Bench, AIME 2026, and HMMT 2026 all showing Mobius ahead of the Transformer at matched quality, and average output length shrinks between one point two and five times depending on benchmark, one point five times shorter on MMLU Pro and AIME 2026, about five times shorter on GPQA Diamond. The step-aligned case study is the most convincing artifact: on an MMLU-Pro linear-algebra question where both models answer correctly, Mobius spends five hundred sixteen tokens against Qwen3.5's two thousand three hundred sixty-four, mostly by eliminating repeated derivations and final checks that consumed eleven hundred forty-seven tokens in the baseline; a second biology example shows four hundred seventy-two versus one thousand six hundred forty-nine tokens, with token counts computed under the Qwen3.5 tokenizer for fairness and counted sequentially over raw predictions so they sum exactly to output length. Layerwise probing adds mechanistic support: intermediate-layer predictions align better with targets, latent iterations progressively refine subsequent-position predictions, and a five-token speculative draft is accepted in full where the baseline loses its draft at position three.

The position sections will interest infrastructure people even if you never train this. On hardware co-design: knowledge-reasoning separation permits keeping reasoning-dedicated parameters resident in GPU memory while storing knowledge parameters largely on SSD with high-priority retrieval, which dense Transformers cannot contemplate because they commingle both in the same parameter set and must load nearly everything at deployment. On self-evolution: decoupled architectures can in principle expand knowledge storage without rewriting reasoning weights, mitigating the catastrophic forgetting that end-to-end retraining causes, since today a Transformer acquires any new skill only by altering knowledge and reasoning simultaneously. On world models: native latent reasoning offers a continuous-space prior that token-level Transformers lack, the authors arguing that modeling continuous physical spaces with discrete tokens would demand petabyte-scale parameter counts where terabyte-scale language models already strain existing hardware. On scientific discovery: internalizing deliberation into differentiable latent space is offered as substrate for something like intuition, while flattening all knowledge into one addressable repository raises the odds of compositional generalization across domains. All of these are framed honestly as unvalidated hypotheses requiring joint work on data, infrastructure, and training algorithms.

Skeptical ledger, itemized. Continual pre-training experiments lack a control: converting Qwen3.5 to Mobius and adding one trillion more tokens confounds architecture change with additional training, so the benchmark gains cannot be attributed cleanly. The from-scratch comparison covers only one scale, one tokenizer-free benchmark family, MMLU, and one trillion tokens; expert-activation analysis shows continual pre-training inherits block-diagonal routing priors from the source checkpoint, meaning the converted model may not exploit shared memory fully. Whether shorter chains reflect genuine internalized computation or merely compressed answers remains partially open, though the step-aligned traces argue the former. No code or inference-kernel details appear for the four-times figure beyond batch-size curves, so reproduce throughput on your own serving stack before planning around it. Bottom line: this is the most substantive public attempt yet to break the knowledge-reasoning coupling in production-scale models, with real efficiency numbers behind it. Verify the conversion recipe and serving claims independently before betting a roadmap on it, but the direction has earned attention.
