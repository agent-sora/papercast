AI4AI at Test-Time: Strong-to-Weak Capability Transfer via Harnesses, by Cheng Qian, Wenting Zhao, and Liangwei Yang, from Salesforce AI Research and the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign. The question is clean: can a strong builder model write inference-time scaffolding that lifts a weak target model without touching a single parameter? The answer across 72 controlled runs is yes, dramatically. GPT-5.4-mini's macro accuracy on four Theory-of-Mind benchmarks goes from 0.488 to a best of 0.912, and the mechanism analysis tells you exactly where the gains come from, which is what makes this paper useful rather than just another leaderboard entry.

Setup first, because the protocol is what makes the result credible. Four benchmarks aggregate into a hidden test set of 3,900 items: BigToM with 1,200 binary belief, goal, and action questions; Hi-ToM with 1,200 nested belief questions across recursion orders zero through four with deception and multi-room tracking; MMToM-QA with 600 Bayesian goal and belief inference items from action traces; MuMA-ToM with 900 three-choice multi-agent belief questions. Each builder gets only a 195-item validation slice, 5 percent drawn by fixed seed, plus a rule file and a demo showing how to call the target. The builder iterates inside an agentic coding platform, Cursor, Claude Code, or GPT Codex, proposes or revises scaffold code, evaluates on validation, diagnoses errors, repeats, then exports an executable entry point. A human evaluator runs that entry point on the full hidden set afterward. No leakage path.

Eleven builder configurations were tested, from GPT-5.5, Opus-4.7 at four reasoning-effort tiers, Sonnet-4.6, Codex-5.3, Gemini-3.1-Pro, Gemini-3.5-flash, down to Grok-0.1, each repeated three times. Baselines: vanilla direct calling at 0.488 for GPT-5.4-mini and 0.761 for Gemini-3.5-flash, and UserHarness, a human-engineered ToM harness, reaching 0.939 on the same weak backbone.

Headline results. All 57 scaffolded GPT-5.4-mini runs beat baseline; mean macro accuracy is 0.763, up 0.275. The best run, GPT-5.5 on GPT Codex, hits 0.912, an 87 percent relative uplift, statistically decisive by paired McNemar over 3,900 items with chi-squared above 10,000. That scaffold fixes 1,717 baseline errors while breaking 105 previously correct ones. On BigToM the automated scaffold reaches 1.00, actually beating UserHarness at 0.95. It still trails the human harness on Hi-ToM, 0.80 versus 0.87, MMToM-QA, 0.84 versus 0.98, and MuMA-ToM, 0.88 versus 0.96. So automation matches human engineering exactly where the task compiles into rules and lags elsewhere.

The technique taxonomy is the implementation goldmine. Format enforcement appears in 57 of 57 scaffolds, greedy decoding in 56, benchmark routing in 54, forced chain-of-thought in 45, polarity and negation logic in 45. Deterministic solvers appear in only 31, few-shot examples in 12, verification passes in 7, self-consistency voting in 3. Read that as disciplined task engineering beating exotic inference tricks: nearly universal reliability controls prevent malformed outputs and sampling variance, then polarity logic, structured belief-state extraction, and deterministic solving provide the actual separation between strong and weak builders.

Three analysis results deserve emphasis. First, determinism fraction, the share of the 3,900 items answered entirely by code or structured rules, correlates with final accuracy at r equals 0.72, and offloadability varies sharply by benchmark: about 0.94 for BigToM, 0.51 for Hi-ToM, 0.44 for MMToM-QA, 0.36 for MuMA-ToM. Second, builder reasoning effort is a monotone lever. Opus-4.7 pooled accuracy climbs from 0.711 at low effort to 0.793, 0.807, and 0.856 at extra-high, Spearman rho 0.77, with scaffold size growing from roughly 510 to 650 lines at low effort to 1,000 to 1,300 at extra-high. Third, validation budget does not matter. Median validation probes per run is five; iteration count correlates with final performance at just r equals 0.17, while best validation score tracks it at r equals 0.96 with mean optimism gap of only 0.021. Builder quality dominates probing quantity.

Platform effects are second-order and conditional. Matched native-versus-Cursor comparisons average just +0.013 with no statistical reliability, permutation p of 0.48, and reverse for some builders like Sonnet-4.6. The interaction worth knowing: Claude Code trails Cursor at low reasoning effort by 0.034 but leads once effort reaches medium or higher. Pooled platform rankings mostly reflect which builders ran there, not causal advantage.

Target-model analysis produces the headroom law: realized uplift correlates with one minus baseline accuracy at r equals 0.75 across all builder-benchmark-target combinations. Scaffolding recovers latent competence, format compliance, observation tracking, recursive state, rather than creating new capability. Against the stronger Gemini-3.5-flash baseline of 0.761, gains shrink to +0.110 on average, concentrate almost entirely on BigToM, and every builder regresses on at least one benchmark, nine of twenty matched cases, mostly where baseline already sits near ceiling. Over-scaffolding is real: past a certain competence level, extra routing and rules break more correct answers than they fix. Builders adapt, shifting model-only handling on MuMA-ToM from 40 to 73 percent when the target is stronger. Self-scaffolding, GPT-5.4-mini building for itself, yields +0.17 to +0.22, so a strong builder is not necessary for uplift but is necessary for the top regime, +0.31 versus +0.17 on Codex. Top scaffolds repair overlapping but non-identical error sets whose union covers 97 percent of baseline errors.

Residual errors mark the uncompilable core: Hi-ToM falls from essentially perfect at order zero to 0.700 at recursion order four, deception costs another 6 points, and MMToM-QA type-two Bayesian goal-inference subtypes sit near 0.68. What remains stubbornly hard is nested belief under deception and Bayesian inference from ambiguous traces.

Skeptical read: four ToM benchmarks are unusually rule-exposed, so treat 0.912 as an upper bound for how far harness compilation travels; benchmark-specific routing flirts with teaching to the test, though the validation-only protocol means it generalizes across instances legitimately. Run variance exists, standard deviation 0.036 within cells with a worst-case spread of 0.201, concentrated in deterministic-solver strategies where one logic bug swings hundreds of items, so build two or three candidate scaffolds and pick by validation. Bottom line: this is the most systematic evidence yet that capability transfer can happen through code rather than weights, and the recipe, universal format enforcement, routing, then compile whatever structure the task exposes into deterministic solvers, transfers directly to your own evaluation pipelines. Budget builder reasoning effort, keep validation slices small, and gate scaffolding by measured headroom before you wrap a model that is already strong.
