WeMM-Embedding: WeChat Multi-Modal Embedding Technical Report, by Junjie Zhou, Ke Mei, and Lei Li — three co-first authors at WeChat Vision, part of Tencent. This is a family of universal multimodal embedding models at two, four, and nine billion parameters, built on natively multimodal Qwen3.5 backbones. The headline claims: the two-billion variant already beats prior leading eight-billion open models on the MMEB-v2 benchmark suite, and the nine-billion model posts an overall score of eighty point six, first on the official leaderboard as of August twenty-fourth, twenty twenty-six. Unlike many embedding reports, this one ships with production evidence — fourteen online A/B tests across WeChat recommendation and search, all consistent enough to roll out.

Start with the representation interface, because it drives everything downstream. Any input is an optional task instruction followed by an ordered sequence of text, image, and video segments. The model appends a dedicated embedding token to the sequence and takes that token's final hidden state, L-two normalized, as the vector. Because attention stays causal and native, you can insert several embedding tokens at different positions and pull out multiple representations from one forward pass — their worked example places one token after a video's frames and another after its speech transcript, yielding a video-only vector and a joint video-plus-transcript vector simultaneously. Matryoshka Representation Learning handles dimension flexibility: truncate the vector to any supported width, renormalize, done, with all widths computable from a single forward pass. The release also includes the data construction pipeline and inference stack, so a team could in principle reproduce the family end to end rather than merely download weights. For engineers sizing vector indexes, their ablation says two hundred fifty-six dimensions retains ninety-eight point seven percent of full-quality image and video performance, while visual documents degrade faster — dense text is the information-heavy case — and retrieval tasks are the most dimension-sensitive overall.

The training recipe is two stages over a unified pair format. Every example becomes a source instance, a target, optional explicit hard negatives, and an optional graded relevance score, regardless of modality or task. Stage one runs on several hundred million such pairs spanning weakly supervised web pairs, captions, retrieval queries, classification reformulated as source-to-label-name matching, multimodal question answering, and graded-relevance data. Optimization is standard contrastive learning with a learnable temperature for ordinary pairs, plus a score-gap-weighted ranking objective for graded pairs that weights each comparison by how far apart the human relevance labels sit. One engineering detail matters more than it looks: batches are drawn from a single dataset so the in-batch negatives share the task's candidate space. In their stage-one ablation, mixing tasks within batches costs three point four points overall — the largest single factor they tested — while removing task-specific instructions costs zero point eight and removing near-duplicate masking of colliding targets costs half a point.

Stage two is where the craft concentrates, on a curated set about one tenth the size of the big collection. Curation has three parts. First, semantic resampling: encode each pair with an intermediate checkpoint, fit a three-level residual quantizer, and downsample examples falling into densely populated codes — flattening frequency skew without forcing a uniform distribution. Second, quality filtering with a multimodal language model that checks whether each pair actually expresses its intended matching relation, and rewrites factually wrong alt-text while keeping its style. Third, hard-negative enrichment: generated plausible-but-wrong text targets, and retrieved near-miss images or videos mined by the checkpoint model itself.

Stage two adds two supervision channels beyond contrastive learning, and the report is refreshingly honest about one of them. Dedicated rerankers score query-specific candidate lists, replacing human relevance levels with model scores in the same gap-weighted ranking loss — but the authors state plainly that reranker supervision did not consistently help across multimodal tasks, delivering stable gains only on a narrow subset, so they restricted it to those settings. In the cumulative two-billion ablation it contributes just a tenth of a point. The bigger lever is cross-scale distillation: the frozen nine-billion model serves as an embedding teacher for the two- and four-billion students, transferring whole batch-wise similarity distributions rather than one-hot positives, in both source-to-target and target-to-source directions. That single move adds roughly zero point nine points — the largest stage-two contribution — which they attribute to softer, structure-preserving targets. With no larger teacher available for the nine-billion flagship, they train several specialized variants under complementary mixtures and merge them, a pragmatic acknowledgment that self-distillation was not on the menu.

Now the results, with the caveats stated where the paper states them. On MMEB-v2's seventy-eight datasets covering images, video, and visual documents: the two-billion model scores seventy-seven point nine overall, four point seven above the strongest prior two-billion open model and marginally above the eight-billion version of that same baseline family; four billion reaches seventy-nine point two; nine billion tops out at eighty point six, ahead of every listed open and proprietary entry including closed-source leaderboard submissions without public weights. On MMEB-v3, which extends to one hundred ninety tasks including text retrieval, agent-oriented tool and interface retrieval, and audio: their two-billion scores fifty-six against fifty point nine for the comparable baseline, and note the protocol — unsupported audio tasks count as zero for these models, which do not take audio input, so the flagship's audio column is honestly empty rather than quietly excluded. Cross-modal retrieval against commercial systems uses figures reported in the Gemini Embedding Two study: the nine-billion model averages eighty-one point seven across twelve benchmarks versus seventy-nine point five for Gemini Embedding Two, with the largest margins in text-to-video retrieval. Their readme-plus-reproducibility stance also matters: weights, code, and construction pipeline are all public on Hugging Face and GitHub under the Tencent organization, an unusually complete release for an industrial embedding family.

The deployment evidence deserves equal billing because offline benchmarks routinely overstate production value. Internally, a twenty-six-task WeChat benchmark shows seventy-two point zero versus sixty point nine for the strongest open comparison. In production the embeddings feed candidate retrieval, ranking features, user sequence modeling, and cross-domain matching across Channels, Official Accounts, Moments, and e-commerce, and derived semantic identifiers serve as compact discrete index keys. Fourteen consecutive online A/B tests showed consistent gains that were rolled out, with the largest effects on long-tail and newly published content — exactly where noisy embeddings usually fail hardest. In search the same vectors support unimodal and cross-modal retrieval across Channel videos, Official Account articles, and Moments content. The team also derives semantic identifiers — compact discrete codes induced from the embeddings — used directly as indexing keys and sequence-modeling features, letting symbolic recommenders consume the representation without rebuilding around dense vectors alone. Supported Matryoshka widths run from sixty-four up to two thousand forty-eight dimensions, all obtainable from one forward pass.

What would I re-verify before adopting? First, "first on the leaderboard" is dated to August twenty-fourth and several entries above it are closed submissions with undisclosed sizes; treat the proprietary comparisons as reported-by-others numbers. Second, the visual-document weakness under dimension reduction suggests keeping full vectors for document workloads. Third, three of the four biggest training tricks — semantic-ID resampling, teacher distillation, task-consistent batching — are cheap to copy, but the curation pipeline leans on an internal multimodal language model whose error profile is not characterized anywhere in the report. Bottom line: this is one of the more complete embedding releases this year — real scales, honest ablations including a negative result on reranking, and production A/B evidence few academic groups can match; the two-billion variant is arguably the story, since it matches last generation's eight-billion flagships at a quarter the size. Worth noting for anyone planning an analogous scaling run: the reported two-billion-to-nine-billion gap on the hardest retrieval categories is narrow — visual-document retrieval moves only from sixty-three point four to sixty-seven point four, video retrieval from seventy-one point seven to seventy-seven point seven — so expected returns above the small-model tier concentrate in long-tail coverage rather than headline averages.
