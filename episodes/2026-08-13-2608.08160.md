Can LLM Agents Stick to the Script? A Benchmark for Long-Horizon Consistency in Interactive Narratives, by Yingpeng Ma, Jianhao Yan, and Bei Shi, from the NLP2CT Lab at the University of Macau, Westlake University, Harbin Institute of Technology Shenzhen, the University of Cambridge, and the University of Aberdeen. The task is Narrative Commitment Preservation: an agent narrating an interactive story must keep established facts fixed, honor ordering and invariant constraints, acknowledge player actions, and still advance the plot across a hundred turns. If that sounds soft, note the authors' own framing applies directly to coding agents, where a new request must not silently break implemented behavior, interface contracts, tests, or safety requirements. Same structure, different skin.

The benchmark construction starts from the CMU Movie Summary Corpus. Human experts clean the candidate pool of duplicate records, mismatched characters, and incomplete synopses, then manually select 100 movies to maximize genre spread across 18 categories, action and science fiction leading at 13 and 12 percent, comedy 11, western 9, fantasy 8, crime 7. Each environment pairs a synopsis with a specific in-story character designated as the player role, not necessarily a protagonist, and every annotation obeys point-of-view discipline: only facts that character could plausibly observe or know at that point in the story, with explicit future-sight isolation on the initial state preventing omniscient leakage. Genre tags are manually annotated as primary plus secondary per movie. Three experts later audited all 100 specifications for ambiguities that could affect evaluation; seven were flagged by exactly one reviewer each, all localized rather than systematic construction errors.

Each environment ships a structured specification with three parts. The initial fact ledger fixes world state at time zero, including negative knowledge such as what the player does not yet know, and initializes the entities needed to interpret commitments: relationships, locations, possessions. The commitment set holds fixed narrative rules in three types: invariants that must stay true over a story span, like remaining undercover until exposed; orderings requiring one event before another, like betrayal occurring only after alliance formation; and achievements that must complete for success, like obtaining the evidence. Invariants and orderings are checked for violations while achievements are tracked for satisfaction; success requires all achievements satisfied with no violation anywhere. The reference trajectory is an ordered sequence of plot steps, each defined by a trigger event and a key lasting state delta. During interaction the active ledger gains new persistent facts and marks outdated ones negated, retaining negated entries for auditability while excluding them from active state.

Evaluation separates the narrator under test from a fixed auditing protocol run each turn through four steps. Conflict Check scans the narrator's response for fact conflicts against the ledger, commitment conflicts such as breaking ordering constraints or invariants, and player-input conflicts where the response ignores what the player did; the narrator may block or redirect an action but must acknowledge it and explain the outcome. Candidate fact updates extracted from a response commit to the ledger only if no confirmed conflict exists, and any initially detected conflict gets secondary confirmation before terminating anything, guarding against auditor false positives. Fact Update commits extracted additions and negations only after that check passes. Trajectory Node Update advances the plot index only when both trigger event and key delta are explicitly complete; ambiguous cases never advance. Commitment Check tracks each rule as pending or satisfied with cited evidence referencing specific facts or trajectory nodes. Interactions terminate as CONFLICT on confirmed violation, SURVIVAL at the hundred-turn cap, or SUCCESS when all achievement commitments satisfy cleanly. An adversarial player agent generates first-person inputs without meta commentary while withheld from hidden facts, commitments, and future trajectory nodes, so it pressures the narrator strictly from inside the story's information horizon: accusing too early, rushing ahead of prerequisites, probing world limits.

Results across six narrator backbones, GPT-5.2, GPT-4o-mini, DeepSeek-V3.2, Qwen3-235B-A22B, Kimi-K2.5, Grok-4.1-Fast, with Gemini-2.5-Flash as adversarial player and default auditor, temperature 0.6 top-p 0.95 and maximum generation length about 8,000 tokens, JSON-only outputs validated per call with up to two retries of just the failed model call before invalidation, are blunt. After twenty turns even GPT-5.2 survives only 42 percent of environments; most models approach zero well before the cap. Across all 600 runs, six models times 100 movies, conflict-free completion to turn 100 occurs in 3.5 percent, and those rare survivors usually still miss achievement commitments. Average turns: GPT-5.2 leads at 32.9, GPT-4o-mini 24.8, Qwen3 16.8, DeepSeek 15.9, Grok 7.9, Kimi 2.9. Interesting split: DeepSeek advances furthest per turn, 15.4 percent trajectory progress and 13.4 percent commitments satisfied versus GPT-5.2's more conservative 9.9 and 11.2, suggesting aggressive pacing reaches later plot nodes before failing while conservative pacing extends life without advancing. Nobody exceeds 14 percent satisfaction.

Failure decomposition puts fact conflicts first everywhere, 40 percent even for GPT-5.2 up to 68 percent for Qwen3. Qualitative categories: hallucinating against the ledger, like moving a character from bridge to doorway without explanation; triggering unknown facts, revealing the villain before the story supports knowing; forcibly changing reality, erasing a completed player action; ignoring player input outright. A memory-augmented baseline, HiAgent built on GPT-4o-mini, extends average turns from 22 to 30 and cuts commitment conflicts from 26 to 4 percent, but fully-satisfied runs drop from two to zero and player-input conflicts jump from 13 to 38 percent, because its memory compression abstracts concrete player actions away. Memory helps duration and hurts fidelity. Under cooperative rather than adversarial play, average turns nearly double to 46 and limit-reaching runs go from three to nineteen, yet zero runs satisfy all achievements and most interactions still end in conflict. Difficulty is structural, not adversarial artifact.

Auditor robustness got real scrutiny. Three checker backbones, GPT-5.4-mini, Gemini-2.5-Flash, and GPT-5.2, produce consistent conflict profiles evaluating the same GPT-4o-mini narrator: fact rates 60, 64, and 67 percent; commitment rates 26, 32, and 41; player-input rates 13, 11, and 15. Pairwise Pearson correlations run 0.987, 0.963, and 0.986 with Spearman 0.922, 0.903, and 0.982, so aggregate conclusions do not hinge on one evaluator backbone. Strictness varies though: GPT-5.4-mini and Gemini still allow a few successful runs, five each in that slice counting limit-reachers plus full satisfiers, while GPT-5.2 as auditor allows zero, so absolute numbers shift with auditor choice. Human verification: experts reviewed 100 final results for GPT-4o-mini and disputed only four fact-conflict false positives, all boundary cases of state change or epistemic update, with zero disputes on commitment or player-input judgments.

The diagnosis section names three architectural causes: no explicit state tracking, forcing reconstruction of world state from dialogue history at every turn with drift and contradiction accumulating over long horizons; next-token training objectives that do not penalize logical inconsistency when output stays linguistically fluent; and accommodation bias, models yielding to conflicting player claims because perceived helpfulness outranks logical integrity. The authors stress logical consistency is necessary but not sufficient for good narrative; they target it first because even this baseline layer is unsolved. Limitations acknowledged: single-threaded chronological narratives only, extending to branching timelines, flashbacks, or parallel perspectives needs temporal-scope generalization of commitments and facts; provider-side nondeterminism prevents exact replication despite fixed prompts and JSON enforcement; and one specific adversarial strategy represents just one intervention pattern real users might not match.

Skeptical read: LLM-audited evaluation inherits judge limitations despite the confirmation steps and human spot-checks, and 100 movies is small enough that genre-level conclusions are thin. Success requires satisfying every achievement, so near-misses score as failures; a partial-credit metric would show a different curve. Still, the protocol itself, fact ledger plus typed commitments plus trajectory nodes with point-of-view isolation, is immediately portable to any long-horizon agent domain. Bottom line: if your agents hold obligations across long interactive sessions, this benchmark says treat consistency as the primary bottleneck, since frontier models lose coherent state within dozens of turns and memory augmentation trades one failure mode for another. Reuse the auditing scaffold on your own workflows before trusting any vendor's long-horizon claims.
