Embodied-Navigator: Point, Think, Memorize, and Align for Efficient Navigation. First authors Hongyan Feng, Sunlai Chen, and Xuanyu Liu, from the OmniAl Group of the ACES Lab at Zhejiang University together with the Zhejiang Humanoid Robot Innovation Center. The system is called TAMP-Nav: Point, Think, Memorize, Align. It reports 66.2 percent success on R2R-CE validation-unseen with a tenth of the training trajectories of some competitors, and it runs inference in about half the time per task. Those efficiency numbers are the real story here.

Three bottlenecks motivate the design. First, the geometric gap: vision-language models are pretrained on 2D image-text pairs, but vision-and-language navigation forces them to emit low-level atomic actions like turn left thirty degrees or to regress 3D coordinates, which demands implicit geometric transformations they were never trained to perform and produces spatial hallucinations. Second, the reasoning-efficiency dilemma: chain-of-thought improves planning but existing models trigger it at every step or at fixed intervals, multiplying latency. Third, memory: keeping full visual history causes attention dilution and overflow, while discarding history loses critical information, and most architectures carry no explicit spatio-temporal coordinates for what they do keep.

The four components answer those directly. Point is a pixel-to-3D action formulation: the model selects a 2D pixel in an egocentric view—choosing among multiple camera views—and that pixel is projected into 3D using depth and handed to a SLAM controller for low-level execution. In simulation the ground-truth depth map is available; for deployment they use a depth camera. This keeps all geometric reasoning out of the network and inside classical geometry where it belongs.

Think and Memorize is selective reasoning plus anchor-trajectory memory. Deep chain-of-thought triggers only at critical topological nodes; those states are stored as Explicit Anchors holding three things: a space-time indicator token, raw visual features retained for loop-closure-style matching, and a planning beacon summarizing task phase from the reasoning text. Routine intervals between anchors get compressed into Space-Time Indicators alone—single tokens encoding position x and y, temporal index t, and orientation yaw. The encoding detail matters for implementation: spatial coordinates use 2D rotary position embeddings, time uses 1D RoPE, and yaw is encoded as its sine and cosine rather than the raw angle specifically to avoid the discontinuity at the zero-to-360-degree boundary; concatenated features pass through an MLP into the model's hidden dimension. Yaw handling like this is the kind of thing you will get wrong on the first attempt if you skip their appendix.

Align is a two-level GRPO stage with multi-branch rollouts. For each instruction the system samples eight complete navigation trajectories. Rewards stack two levels: local step rewards cover target approach, collision avoidance, stop-action correctness, reasoning value—which correlates chain-of-thought indicators with approach scores—and format adherence; global trajectory rewards cover task success, SPL efficiency, and a reasoning-density penalty that decays steeply above a 0.4 chain-of-thought ratio and reaches zero at 0.6. Advantages are computed at both trajectory and step level, so the model learns when thinking pays, not just where to walk.

Training data comes first. MultiNav-CoT contains 90,000 trajectories filtered from VLN-CE, excluding rendering artifacts and Gemini-assessed low-quality instructions. Key nodes for supervision are mined with a three-step procedure: score each frame by CLIP semantic relevance to the instruction plus visual-change magnitude, apply distance-based greedy filtering enforcing a minimum spacing between selected nodes, then pad temporally wherever gaps exceed a maximum threshold, yielding key nodes over roughly 30 percent of each trajectory. Structured chain-of-thought annotations are generated by Gemini 2.5 Flash decomposed into three separate prompts—task phase localization, observation analysis, future action reasoning—then fused, which reduces hallucination relative to one-shot generation. Base model: Qwen2.5-VL-7B with a brief supervised cold start before GRPO.

Results. On R2R-CE validation-unseen: 66.2 percent success rate, 74.5 oracle success, 58.8 SPL. On RxR-CE: 65.7 success, 56.9 SPL—state of the art across metrics against baselines including StreamVLN, DualVLN, and NavFoM. Efficiency claims check out in the table: 90,000 training trajectories and roughly 700,000 interactions versus DualVLN's 763,000 trajectories and NavFoM's 3.37 million interactions. Average interaction steps drop to 9 per episode versus roughly 30 for DualVLN and StreamVLN, because pointing plus SLAM compresses movement. Inference averages 16.58 seconds per task on a single A800 against 37.47 for StreamVLN and 41.46 for DualVLN. Long-horizon robustness holds: on 5,927 trajectories with expert paths over 12.5 meters, TAMP-Nav reaches 49.8 percent success versus 41.9 for DualVLN and 30.9 for StreamVLN.

The emergent-reasoning analysis is the part I find most convincing. After RL, the fraction of reasoning steps spent in straight corridors falls from 38 percent under SFT to 11 percent, and kernel-density heatmaps of think-token locations concentrate at crossroads, doorways, and near targets. Intervention study: dense chain-of-thought achieves 66.8 percent success at 100 percent reasoning ratio; fixed one-in-three triggers manage 60.1 percent at 36.2 percent ratio; the autonomous policy hits 66.2 percent at only a 26.3 percent ratio. The agent nearly matches always-thinking while skipping almost three quarters of the reasoning.

Ablations isolate each piece under matched budgets. Replacing pixel-to-3D with a NavFoM-style metric waypoint output costs 24.8 points of success on R2R-CE at the SFT stage. Full two-level GRPO beats global-trajectory-only advantage by 6.9 points, and beats the version without annealed guidance by 2.8. Removing space-time indicators from the memory costs 2.6 points. Depth noise testing injects multiplicative Gaussian noise into the selected pixel's depth: success drops only 2.8 points at a noise sigma of 0.2, meaning 16 percent relative error, which the authors attribute to closed-loop self-correction.

Limitations, honestly stated in the paper. The system depends on depth sensing and a reliable SLAM stack; odometry drift and dynamic environments degrade it beyond what the noise test captures. GRPO uses privileged simulator signals for success, SPL, progress, and collision—there is no online RL on hardware, only zero-shot transfer of the frozen policy. The STI token encodes planar position and yaw but not height, so floors are indistinguishable. Reasoning-density thresholds at 0.4 and 0.6 are tuned to R2R-style tasks. Key-node mining may under-select when decision points are visually non-salient.

Implementation guidance: trust the ablation direction—pixel-to-3D is doing heavy lifting—but re-verify inference latency on your own hardware since the 16.58-second figure is A800-specific. Gotchas: replicate the sine-cosine yaw encoding, budget for eight-rollout GRPO groups during training, and expect to retune the density thresholds for any environment unlike Matterport scenes.

Bottom line: TAMP-Nav's contribution is less any single component than the demonstrated division of labor—VLM points, geometry executes, RL decides when to think. The 66.2 percent with 26 percent reasoning ratio is the number to beat.
