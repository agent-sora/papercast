Embodied-Navigator: Point, Think, Memorize, and Align for Efficient Navigation. First authors Hongyan Feng, Sunlai Chen, and Xuanyu Liu, from the OmniAl Group of the ACES Lab at Zhejiang University together with the Zhejiang Humanoid Robot Innovation Center. The system is called TAMP-Nav: Point, Think, Memorize, Align. It reports 66.2 percent success on R2R-CE validation-unseen with a tenth of the training trajectories of some competitors, and it runs inference in about half the time per task. Those efficiency numbers are the real story here.

Three bottlenecks motivate the design. First, the geometric gap: vision-language models are pretrained on 2D image-text pairs, but vision-and-language navigation forces them to emit low-level atomic actions like turn left thirty degrees or to regress 3D coordinates, which demands implicit geometric transformations they were never trained to perform and produces spatial hallucinations with poor sample efficiency. Second, the reasoning-efficiency dilemma: chain-of-thought improves high-level planning but existing models trigger it at every step or at fixed intervals, multiplying inference latency for marginal gains. Third, memory: keeping full visual history causes attention dilution and overflow, while discarding history or decaying it loses critical information, and most architectures carry no explicit spatio-temporal coordinates organizing what they keep.

The four components answer those directly. Point is a pixel-to-3D action formulation: the model selects a 2D pixel in an egocentric view—choosing among multiple camera views—and that pixel is projected into 3D using depth and handed to a SLAM controller that handles local movement. In simulation the ground-truth depth map is available; for deployment they use a depth camera. This keeps all geometric reasoning out of the network and inside classical geometry where it belongs. It also changes what supervision the VLM needs: rather than learning coordinate regressions across camera geometries, it performs visual grounding—select the view, select the pixel—which sits squarely inside its 2D pretraining distribution.

Think and Memorize is selective reasoning plus anchor-trajectory memory. Deep chain-of-thought triggers only at critical topological nodes; those states are stored as Explicit Anchors holding three things: a space-time indicator token, raw visual features retained for loop-closure-style matching against later observations, and a planning beacon—a semantic summary of navigation progress derived from the reasoning text. Routine intervals between anchors get compressed into Space-Time Indicators alone: single tokens encoding position x and y, temporal index t, and orientation yaw. The encoding detail matters for implementation: spatial coordinates use 2D rotary position embeddings, time uses 1D RoPE, and yaw is encoded as its sine and cosine rather than the raw angle specifically to avoid the discontinuity at the zero-to-360-degree boundary; concatenated features pass through an MLP projecting into the model's native hidden dimension. At each decision step the working context concatenates two uniformly sampled frames between the current step and the most recent anchor with the long-term topological memory of anchors and indicator streams.

Align is a two-level GRPO stage with multi-branch rollouts. For each instruction the system samples eight complete navigation trajectories instead of following expert demonstrations. Rewards stack two levels. Local step rewards cover target approach, collision avoidance ensuring physical traversability, stop-action correctness, reasoning value—which correlates chain-of-thought indicators with approach scores so useless thinking gets penalized—and format adherence covering valid spatial constraints and JSON output. Global trajectory rewards cover task success with relaxed oracle boundaries, SPL efficiency, and a reasoning-density bonus that decays steeply above a 0.4 chain-of-thought ratio and reaches zero at 0.6. Advantages are computed at both trajectory and step levels across the rollout group, so the model learns when thinking pays, not just where to walk. Weight coefficients for both reward sums are given in the appendix, and the reasoning-value reward is what makes selective reasoning learnable at all: without correlating think-token emission with subsequent approach scores, GRPO would treat reasoning as free text with no gradient signal on its timing.

Training data comes first. MultiNav-CoT contains 90,000 trajectories filtered from VLN-CE, excluding rendering artifacts like black images and Gemini-assessed low-quality instructions. Key nodes for supervision are mined with a three-step procedure: score each frame by CLIP semantic relevance to the instruction plus visual-change magnitude computed from visual feature differences, apply distance-based greedy filtering enforcing minimum spacing between selected nodes to guarantee reasoning sparsity, then pad temporally by inserting the highest-scoring intermediate frame wherever gaps exceed the maximum threshold, yielding key nodes over roughly 30 percent of each trajectory. Structured chain-of-thought annotations are generated by Gemini 2.5 Flash decomposed into three separate prompts—task phase localization, current observation analysis, future action reasoning—then fused in a final pass, which reduces hallucination relative to one-shot generation and supplies the SFT cold start that solves the RL warmup problem. Base model: Qwen2.5-VL-7B with brief supervised training before GRPO.

Results. On R2R-CE validation-unseen: 66.2 percent success rate, 74.5 oracle success, 58.8 SPL, navigation error 3.85 meters. On RxR-CE validation-unseen: 65.7 success, 56.9 SPL, 72.4 nDTW—state of the art across metrics against baselines including StreamVLN, DualVLN, and NavFoM. Efficiency claims check out in the tables: 90,000 training trajectories and roughly 700,000 interactions versus DualVLN's 763,000 trajectories and NavFoM's 3.37 million interactions, which the authors attribute to dense-reward RL internalizing logic that pure SFT cannot squeeze from limited data—RL lifts R2R-CE success from 55.7 after SFT to 66.2. Average interaction steps drop to 9 per episode versus roughly 30 for DualVLN and StreamVLN, because pointing plus SLAM compresses movement into fewer decisions. Inference averages 16.58 seconds per task on a single A800 GPU against 37.47 for StreamVLN and 41.46 for DualVLN. Long-horizon robustness holds: on 5,927 trajectories with expert paths over 12.5 meters—more than fifty atomic forward actions—TAMP-Nav reaches 49.8 percent success versus 41.9 for DualVLN and 30.9 for StreamVLN.

The emergent-reasoning analysis is the part I find most convincing. After RL, the fraction of reasoning steps spent in straight corridors falls from 38 percent under SFT to 11 percent, and kernel-density heatmaps of think-token locations concentrate at crossroads, doorways, and near target objects. Intervention study: dense chain-of-thought achieves 66.8 percent success at 100 percent reasoning ratio; fixed one-in-three triggers manage 60.1 percent at a 36.2 percent ratio; the autonomous policy hits 66.2 percent at only a 26.3 percent ratio. The agent nearly matches always-thinking while skipping almost three quarters of the reasoning.

Ablations isolate each piece under matched budgets. Replacing pixel-to-3D with a NavFoM-style metric waypoint output costs 24.8 points of success on R2R-CE and 25.2 on RxR-CE at the SFT stage—the largest single effect in the paper. Full two-level GRPO beats global-trajectory-only advantage by 6.9 points on R2R-CE, and beats the version without annealed guidance by 2.8. Removing space-time indicators from the memory costs 2.6 points; uniform-sampling memory costs 5.4 relative to full anchor-trajectory memory; full-history storage without compression manages only 61.9 against 66.2 while paying far more context. The action-space ablation also reports navigation error: pixel-to-3D reaches 4.88 meters against 7.18 for metric waypoints, so the geometric gap shows up in raw localization accuracy, not just success. Depth robustness testing injects multiplicative Gaussian noise into the selected pixel's depth: success drops only 2.8 points at noise sigma 0.2, meaning 16 percent relative error, attributed to closed-loop self-correction across subsequent observations.

Limitations, honestly stated in the paper. The system depends on depth sensing and a reliable SLAM stack; odometry drift, localization failure, and dynamic environments degrade it beyond what the noise test captures. GRPO uses privileged simulator signals for success, SPL, progress, and collision—there is no online RL on hardware, only zero-shot transfer of the frozen policy, because real rollouts are slow and computing the full reward online would need endpoint annotations or a learned verifier. The space-time-indicator token encodes planar position and yaw but not height, so multi-floor buildings remain ambiguous; task-progress descriptions in anchors partially compensate but do not replace 3D localization. Reasoning-density thresholds at 0.4 and 0.6 are tuned to R2R-style tasks. Key-node mining may under-select when decision points are visually non-salient or instructions deviate from R2R style.

Implementation guidance: trust the ablation direction—pixel-to-3D is doing heavy lifting—but re-verify inference latency on your own hardware since the 16.58-second figure is A800-specific. Gotchas: replicate the sine-cosine yaw encoding exactly, budget for eight-rollout GRPO groups during training, and expect to retune the density thresholds for any environment unlike Matterport scenes.

Bottom line: TAMP-Nav's contribution is less any single component than the demonstrated division of labor—VLM points, geometry executes, RL decides when to think. The 66.2 percent success at a 26 percent reasoning ratio is the number to beat.
