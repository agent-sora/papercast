WeMM-Video, by Changli Tang, Jie Yang, and Tianyi Wang, at Tencent and Tsinghua University, is a family of Chinese-English bilingual omni-modal foundation models that unify text, image, audio, and video in a single architecture, with the specific emphasis on joint audio-visual perception and reasoning. The family spans four scales: a 2 billion, a 4 billion, a 9 billion, and a 35 billion parameter model where only 3 billion are active per token, the mixture-of-experts variant. This is the standing-rule case for today, a new foundation model release, so it gets an episode regardless of its upvote rank, and it is a large one, the flagship being a 35 billion parameter sparse model trained on approximately 1.2 trillion tokens. The team publicly releases the full family along with their evaluation and reproduction pipeline, which is the part that makes this useful to a lab that wants to benchmark its own video understanding stack against a strong open baseline rather than a proprietary API.

The framing of the model is what I would hold onto before the numbers. Omni-modal here means the model is trained to treat text, image, audio, and video as one continuous input stream rather than separate modality-specific heads, and the audio-visual joint reasoning is the explicit target, not an afterthought. That matters because most video understanding models are built on a vision-language backbone where audio is bolted on as a secondary encoder, and the result is a model that can describe what it sees but reasons poorly about what it hears. WeMM-Video's claim is that by training the perception of both channels jointly, the model can answer questions that require both, like identifying a sound source in a scene, or reasoning about whether an action and its audio cue are consistent, which is a different and harder task than captioning a silent video. The bilingual angle, Chinese and English, is a second design choice, aimed at the short-form video and embodied intelligence use cases where the dominant content is in Chinese, so a model that only does English well is missing a large slice of the real data.

The architecture is a hybrid-thinking design, which is the key implementation detail. The model supports two operating modes in the same set of weights: a non-thinking instruct mode for fast, direct responses, and a thinking mode that does explicit step-by-step reasoning before answering. This is the same dual-mode idea that has become standard in recent large language models, where you pay for reasoning tokens only when the task needs them, but applied here to an omni-modal model rather than a pure text model. The practical consequence is that you can run the model cheaply for simple perception, like describing a scene, and switch it into reasoning mode for the harder audio-visual questions, without deploying two separate models. The hybrid attention and the mixture-of-experts in the flagship are what make the 35 billion sparse model practical to serve, since only 3 billion parameters are active per token, which is the standard tradeoff for keeping long-context video processing tractable at inference time.

The training recipe is where the reproducibility value lives. The model is trained on a large-scale multimodal corpus of approximately 1.2 trillion tokens through a progressive pre-training phase, where the model first learns each modality and then the joint audio-visual representations, followed by a multi-stage post-training pipeline. The post-training has three named components: multi-teacher on-policy distillation, which uses several teacher models to supervise the student on-policy so it does not drift from the distribution it will actually see at inference; an anti-repetition DPO stage, which is a direct preference optimization aimed at a specific failure mode, the model repeating itself in long video captions; and a specialist reinforcement learning stage, which pushes specific capability areas that the distillation did not fully cover. The fact that they name the anti-repetition stage separately is a tell that repetition in long-form video captioning was a real and annoying failure mode in their internal evaluation, and it is the kind of implementation detail that is worth copying if you are post-training your own video model, because naive DPO on captioning data can make repetition worse if you are not explicitly penalizing it.

The evaluation is the headline, and it is broad. They report across more than 120 benchmarks, which is a lot, and the omni-modal video results are where the flagship claims state of the art among open-source models. The flagship, the 35 billion A3B model, is reported to substantially outperform proprietary models such as Qwen3.5 Omni Flash and Seed 2.0 Mini, while narrowing the gap to Gemini 3.0 Flash, the strongest proprietary reference. The compact 9 billion model is the other result worth noting, because it is reported to surpass the larger open-source Qwen3 Omni 30 billion A3B instruct model, which is a smaller model beating a larger one, the kind of result that usually comes from a better post-training recipe rather than raw scale, and it is the result a lab should pay attention to if it is trying to match a large proprietary model on a small compute budget. The competitive numbers across general audio, silent video, image, and text tasks are reported as highly competitive rather than leading, which is an honest framing: the model is specialized for omni-modal video, and it does not claim to win on every single-modality benchmark.

The skeptical reading. First, more than 120 benchmarks is a wide net, and the state-of-the-art claim is specifically on the omni-modal video subset, which is the subset the model was designed for; the fact that it is only competitive, not leading, on the single-modality tasks is a reminder that this is a specialized model, and you should not extrapolate the video wins to text or image-only workloads. Second, the proprietary comparisons, Qwen3.5 Omni Flash and Seed 2.0 Mini, are specific snapshots of those models, and the proprietary side updates frequently, so the gap to Gemini 3.0 Flash and the lead over those snapshots should be treated as a point-in-time measurement, not a stable property. Third, and this is the one I would verify before adopting, the 1.2 trillion token corpus and the multi-teacher distillation are described at a high level, and if you are trying to reproduce the recipe rather than use the weights, the exact data mix, the teacher set, and the per-stage hyperparameters are the things you would need, and the release includes the pipeline but you should check how much of the data construction is actually shared versus just the training code. The anti-repetition DPO is the one named ingredient I would try to isolate in a reproduction, because it is the most likely place the 9 billion beating the 30 billion comes from.

For implementation, if you are building a video understanding stack, the direct takeaways are the dual-mode architecture, which you should adopt if you are serving a video model at scale, and the anti-repetition preference stage, which is a cheap fix for a common captioning failure. The reproduction pipeline is the asset to inspect first, because it is what tells you whether the 1.2 trillion token recipe is something you could adapt to your own data, or whether the result is locked to their proprietary corpus. The mixture-of-experts flagship is the reference for what a 35 billion sparse omni-modal model can do, but the 9 billion dense model is the more actionable reference for a lab that does not have the serving budget for a sparse model.

To summarize the bottom line. WeMM-Video is a substantial open-source omni-modal foundation model release, with a clear audio-visual joint reasoning focus, a reproducible multi-stage post-training recipe, and a flagship that claims open-source state of the art on video while a compact model beats a larger open competitor. Its limits are the specialization to video, the point-in-time proprietary comparisons, and the high-level description of the data recipe, so use the released weights as a strong baseline, mine the anti-repetition stage as a reusable technique, and verify the data-sharing scope before counting on full reproduction.
