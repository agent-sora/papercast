LLMEval-Logic: A Solver-Verified Chinese Benchmark for Logical Reasoning of
Large Language Models with Adversarial Hardening, by Ming Zhang, Qiyuan Peng,
Yinxi Wei, and a long co-author list that includes Tao Gui, Zhiheng Xi, and
Xuanjing Huang, from Fudan University and the Tencent Hunyuan Team, with a
School of Philosophy co-author that reflects the domain of the work.

The setting is the state of logical-reasoning benchmarks for language
models, and the paper's critique of that state is the motivation. Much of
the existing work generates benchmark items by reverse construction, where a
formal structure is sampled first and then rendered into natural language,
and the problem with that is that the items inherit template-like phrasing
and formula-shaped discourse, which means a model can often predict the
answer from surface cues without doing the underlying inference, so the
benchmark is measuring pattern matching rather than logical reasoning. A
second problem is that the natural-to-formal annotations are coarse or
unaudited, so even when a model produces a candidate formalization, there is
no rubric for grading whether the logical relations, the stated constraints,
and the query are faithfully encoded. And a third problem is saturation,
where published splits are quickly solved by frontier reasoning models,
leaving little headroom to tell the good models from the great ones.

The benchmark, LLMEval-Logic, addresses all three. The items are forward-
authored rather than reverse-templated, which means a human writer with
coursework in logic embeds formalizable logical structure inside a natural
situation, a set of rules, a schedule, an eligibility condition, an
institutional procedure, rather than back-translating a sampled formula. The
benchmark is in Chinese, which is itself a point, because the majority of
logical-reasoning benchmarks are English, and logical reasoning over
natural-language premises is a language-specific skill to the extent that
the premise language and the model's formalization behavior interact. The
pipeline has five audit stages, forward authoring, expert review, layered
normalization, a Z3-based formal verification step, and rubric construction,
and the Z3 step is the part that earns the "solver-verified" in the title,
because the Z3 solver checks that the formalized premises and the formalized
query actually support the annotated answer under the relevant semantics, so
the gold answer is not just a human judgment, it is a machine-checked
consequence of the formalization. The Z3 stage is more specific than that
simplification suggests. The benchmark dispatches the formalized premises
and the formalized query to the solver in one of three modes, depending on
the task type, which the paper labels as possible, necessary, and enumerate
models, and these are treated as natural-language-processing task labels
mapped to distinct solver modes rather than as strict modal-logic operators,
which is an honest and important framing, because it means the benchmark is
testing whether the model can formalize a natural-language claim into the
right solver call, not whether it can use a particular modal logic, and the
Base items are distributed across the three types so that no single solver
mode dominates the distribution. When a Z3 check fails, the item is repaired and re-
verified, so the released benchmark contains only items whose gold answer is
a confirmed consequence of the formalization, which is the property the
"solver-verified" in the title is claiming.

The release is two paired subsets. The Base subset has two hundred and
forty-six items, each shipped with expert-developed rubric atoms, and the
paper reports one thousand four hundred rubric atoms across the Base items,
broken into logical-relation, stated-constraint, and query-alignment
categories. The Hard subset has one hundred and ninety items, each with a
chain of roughly five counterfactual sub-questions, so the Hard items are
multi-step rather than single-shot, and the paper reports nine hundred and
thirty-eight sub-questions across the Hard subset. The hardening is done
through a closed-loop adversarial workflow, a sequence of agent roles that
rewrites the high-accuracy items, the ones models were getting right, along
a set of structural strategies, and ships only the items that pass both
review and formal verification, so the Hard subset is deliberately the
distribution that separates the frontier models. The hardening itself uses
five structural rewrite strategies applied in the adversarial loop. The
first two rewrite the premises, replacing an explicit logical connective with
a natural-language paraphrase, so the connective is still there logically
but no longer visually salient, and adding a constraint that is locally
satisfied but changes the global feasibility of the answer, so the item
tests whether the model tracks the global state rather than the local
clues. The second two rewrite the question, substituting the target entity
with a near-synonym, so the model cannot rely on lexical overlap between the
question and the answer, and changing the query type, for example from
"which option is possible" to "which option is necessary," so the same
premise set supports different answer sets under different queries. The
fifth strategy is the adversarial mutation that produces the hard variants
of an already-solved Base item, and the closed loop keeps only the mutants
that still pass the Z3 verification and the expert review, so the hardening
raises difficulty under a machine-checked correctness guarantee rather than
by removing the guarantee.

The results are across fourteen frontier large language model variants, run
independently three times. The headline numbers are the ones a reader should
remember. The best model reaches only thirty-seven point five percent on the
Hard item accuracy, which is the number that says the benchmark is not
saturated, and even when the models are given the reference symbols, the
highest joint score on the Z3-plus-rubric formalization axis, which grades
whether the model's own formalization is faithful, reaches only sixty point
one six percent, which is the number that says the natural-to-formal
translation is the harder of the two skills, because getting the answer
right with the symbols handed over is more common than faithfully encoding
the premises in the first place.

Now the skeptical read. The first question is the Chinese-only scope, which
is a strength for the languages it covers and a limitation for the field,
because a reader comparing across logical-reasoning benchmarks has to know
that this one is not directly comparable to the English ones, and the
question is whether the difficulty gap, the thirty-seven point five on Hard,
is a reasoning gap or partly a language gap. The second question is the
adversarial hardening, which is the distinctive mechanism, and the honest
version of that question is whether the hardening, rewriting the items models
got right, is producing genuinely harder reasoning or is producing harder
surface phrasing, because a closed-loop rewriter can in principle increase
ambiguity rather than logical depth, and the rubric atoms are what protect
against that, but a reader should check whether the Hard items' increased
difficulty is in the logic or in the language. The third question is the
Z3 verification coverage, the solver checks the answer against the
formalization, which is a real guarantee, but it is a guarantee about the
formalization, not about the natural-language item, so the guarantee is as
strong as the faithfulness of the human-authored formalization, and the
rubric is the audit of that faithfulness, which is expert-developed but not
machine-checked. And the practical question is scale, two hundred and
forty-six Base items is a solid benchmark but a single distribution, and the
field needs to see the frontier gap hold on a second construction pipeline
before the saturation result is treated as settled.

Bottom line: this is a methodologically careful contribution that addresses
three real weaknesses in logical-reasoning benchmarking at once, template-
free forward authoring, solver-verified gold answers, and adversarial
hardening that keeps the benchmark discriminative, with a two-axis evaluation
that separates getting the answer right from faithfully formalizing the
premises. The open question is whether the reported difficulty gap is a
reasoning gap or partly a language gap, and whether the hardening increases
logical depth or surface difficulty.
