TTPO: Test-Time Policy Optimization, by Aozhe Wang of Zhejiang University and Alibaba Group, Zhengxi Lu of Zhejiang University, and Jianze Wang of Alibaba Group, with further authors from both institutions. The setting is test-time training: a model improves on the very problems it is about to be evaluated on, with no answer key, because labels never arrive. Every strong post-training recipe we have, reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards and on-policy self-distillation, assumes an oracle that grades outputs or hands the teacher the right answer. TTPO's contribution is an asymmetric objective that survives a majority-vote pseudo-label being wrong, which on competition math it usually is, and the empirical claim is that this label-free recipe matches the label-supervised version.

The problem structure first. Sample a group of rollouts per problem, cluster the final answers by mathematical equivalence, and take the largest cluster as the pseudo-label. On AIME 2026 with the Qwen3 one point seven B base model, that pseudo-label is wrong for about eighty-five percent of prompts. The prior approaches all break on this. TTRL uses the pseudo-label as a reward, so a wrong majority reinforces its own error once per trajectory. The dangerous variant is dense supervision: condition the same model on the pseudo-answer to form a teacher, and distill the student toward it. A corrupted reward misleads once per trajectory; a corrupted teacher misleads at every token.

The paper's key observation is that the failure is asymmetric. Even when the pseudo-label is wrong, about seventy-nine percent of the rollouts that disagree with it are wrong too, producing an answer that is neither the majority answer nor the truth. So a penalty on a disagreeing rollout is usually correct whether or not the pseudo-label is correct, because the penalty depends only on the disagreement, never on the content of the majority answer. Distillation toward the pseudo-label has no such tolerance. That single observation dictates the whole architecture: apply each signal only where it stays correct.

TTPO partitions rollouts into positives, which agree with the pseudo-label, and negatives, which do not, then runs two branches. Positives get on-policy self-distillation: the teacher is the same model conditioned on the pseudo-answer, computing per-token forward K L divergence against the student, and the update is weighted per token. The weighting combines two normalized signals, student entropy and teacher-student divergence, with a soft-OR, so distillation concentrates on positions where the student is uncertain or confidently wrong, and fades where the student has already converged. Here is the subtle part that makes the positive branch safe: the teacher is conditioned on the same answer the student itself produced, so even when that answer is wrong, the update reduces to distilling the model's careful thinking mode into its fast non-thinking mode rather than toward an arbitrary external error. Negatives get grouped reinforcement learning: binary rewards from the majority classification, group-relative advantages, which are negative for this set, and a token mask that penalizes only the top half of tokens by a confidence-weighted surprise score, the product of negative log probability and normalized certainty. That masking matters more than it sounds, because in this asymmetric setup G R P O acts only on negative samples, so the usual counterbalancing from positive-advantage gradients is gone, and unmasked penalties would damage locally correct tokens inside failed trajectories. The two losses are combined with a reinforcement weight of zero point one. Training uses sixty-four rollouts per problem, selects eight of them for the gradient step at a fifty-fifty positive-negative split, fine-tunes Qwen3 at one point seven, four, and eight billion parameters with LoRA rank sixty-four on all linear layers, and runs one hundred steps with the peak checkpoint reported from saves every twenty-five steps, and generates at most sixteen thousand tokens per rollout so that answers are never truncated away before extraction. Evaluation is average over twelve runs at temperature one, on AIME 2025 and 2026, HMMT 2025 and 2026, and BRUMO 2025.

Results. In the OpenThoughts setting, where baselines use ground-truth labels and TTPO ignores the labels it is given, TTPO edges out label-supervised self-distillation at every scale: forty point one versus thirty-nine point seven average at one point seven B, fifty-eight point six versus fifty-eight point four at four B, and sixty-two point six versus sixty-one point seven at eight B. In the pure test-time training setting, training directly on the test problems, the one point seven B model goes from thirty-eight point zero to forty-five point two average, ahead of TTRL at forty point two and self-distillation with a greedy self-teacher at forty-one point nine, a seven point two point absolute gain over the base model. The four B model reaches sixty-one point one, which already exceeds the eight B base model at sixty point seven, and the eight B model reaches sixty-five point three. With thinking mode disabled at evaluation, the paper reports gains of twenty-five point two to thirty-six point four percent across scales, which it attributes to the distillation branch explicitly transferring careful reasoning into the fast mode.

The ablations support the design point by point. Removing positive-token weighting costs one to three points depending on the benchmark; removing the negative-token mask costs up to four point seven, and the authors note the two failures are different in kind, diluted gradients versus noise injection. The update-strategy matrix is the most informative table: full TTPO scores forty-eight point nine on AIME 2026 at one point seven B, positive-only distillation gets forty-six point seven, distilling all rollouts toward the pseudo-label gets forty-six point three, negative-only distillation gets forty-three point nine, and assigning G R P O to positives scores thirty-seven point two, which is also what the fully reversed assignment scores. In other words, the assignment direction is doing real work, and getting it backwards is worse than doing nothing. A further ablation on what privileged information the teacher receives finds that a short answer hint with a thinking teacher works best at forty-six point five, while conditioning on a full trajectory degrades to forty-one point one, because the teacher stops reasoning and starts completing a given prefix.

The skeptical read. First, everything here is competition mathematics, five benchmarks in one family, with pass rates low enough that the pseudo-label is wrong most of the time; the method's tolerance is designed for exactly that regime, and nothing in the paper shows it helps, or even holds, where majorities are usually right and dense supervision is mostly clean. Second, the reporting convention is peak across checkpoints, which flatters every method in the comparison equally but means the numbers are best-case, not final-checkpoint. Third, training on the test set is the premise, so the only generalization evidence that counts is the cross-benchmark experiment, where training on one benchmark improves the other two; that result is shown for the one point seven B model only. Fourth, the gains over the strongest baseline are three points at one point seven B and one point six at eight B, real but modest, and the headline forty-five point two rests on the smallest model where the base is weakest. What I would trust: the asymmetry observation itself, which is clean, cheap to verify on your own rollouts, and likely to transfer to any label-free post-training loop. What I would re-verify: whether the token-masking machinery earns its complexity at larger scales, and whether pseudo-label routing really tightens as the model improves, the self-evolution claim, which is asserted from a trend rather than demonstrated over long horizons. Bottom line: TTPO is a careful piece of engineering around one good statistical insight, that disagreement is more reliable information than consensus when consensus is usually wrong, and the numbers show label-free test-time training working about as well as the label-supervised version. For anyone running self-improvement loops without verifiers, the asymmetric assignment of distillation to agreement and penalties to disagreement is the part worth stealing.
